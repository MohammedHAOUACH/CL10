## Abstract

A quantum cellular automaton is defined on a Planck lattice whose $2{\times}2{\times}2$ cells carry the Clifford algebra $\mathrm{Cl}(10,0)$ on eight sub-sites (on-site space $\mathbb{C}^{256}$) and are updated by one six-stage product of $\mathrm{Spin}(10)$ plane rotations and swap-rotations. From this root follow, with no added field: a $(1{+}3)$ Minkowski subalgebra; the $16$-component Weyl module whose charges are exactly one Standard Model generation, all eight gauge and mixed anomalies cancelling; the $\mathbf{45}$ adjoint containing the twelve gauge bosons; the $5/3$ hypercharge normalisation. The step is exactly unitary ($\det U = 1$, $\pm$-paired spectrum, exact matter/antimatter spectral mirror) and strictly causal, with measured front speed exactly one Planck cell per stage. The doubling of the lattice spectrum is displayed, not hidden: twelve gapless nodes at the half-zone momenta, gapped by a Wilson factor of the root's own algebra, with the split-node set an input (O9). The closure axiom — the update must be an element of the root — quantizes the rotation angle to $g \in \{\pi/2,\pi/4\}$: the fundamental step has order $8$, the internal spectrum is the finite algebraic set $\{\pm\pi/4\,(10),\ \pm3\pi/4\,(6)\}$, and the on-site update has zero continuous unfixed numbers. The tensions are named, not hidden: the doubler cones are linear in every sampled direction but anisotropic (velocity-tensor anisotropy $0.748$–$0.758$; $0$ of $2726$ gapless points of the Wilson family reach $A < 10^{-6}$; three nodal planes, not a cone, at the exactly gapless corner multiplet; frame averaging over the ten generators is blind to the anisotropy; and no bond of the $880$-point entangling programme is both gapless and non-commuting — a rigidity wall at gap $8\times10^{-3}$ over the axis-covariant class, which Proposition 6 explains as a parity no-go and escapes outside the class, on a parity-degenerate node that the engine's own six-stage product is searched for and does not reach, Section 8.4), and at the closed angle the gapless set becomes six nodal lines and the per-node chirality bookkeeping must be redone. The gauged bond layer, the gravitational sector and the phenomenology of this root are constructed and audited in companion papers [Paper II, Paper III].

### Summary of results

The determinism claim is interpretive: one time-independent local rule fixes every future state of the lattice — 't Hooft's cellular-automaton determinism exact at the fundamental update — with randomness entering only through the measurement projection; the projection and the Born rule are inputs of the interpretation (O9), not derived from the unitary step. The spin–statistics identification (even sector bosonic, odd sector fermionic) is likewise a postulate of the interpretation, not a derived theorem.

The results of this paper are the kinematics (Theorem 1), the matter content and anomaly freedom (Proposition 1, Theorem 2), the gauge content and the $5/3$ (Theorem 3), the exact unitarity and the causal radius (Section 7, Theorem 5), the spectral mirror (Theorem 4), the doubling and its Wilson gapping (Proposition 2, Section 8.1), the Lorentz structure at the nodes with its measured anisotropy (Section 8.2), the massless-mode census (Section 8.3), the cone no-go and the parity theorem (Propositions 4–6, Section 8.4), and the quantized clock with its measured costs (Theorem 6, Section 9). The loop-factor ladder, the coupling unification, the dark-matter and neutrino audits, and the full provenance audit are carried by Paper III; the gauged bond layer and the lattice gravitational sector by Paper II.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The picture

Two habits of language do most of the misleading. The first calls $c$ the speed of light. Light is one traveller at that speed; the constant is the speed of causality. The second is wave–particle duality, taught as a paradox. On a lattice it dissolves: there is one wave, always; what a detector registers is a single localised event, because the detector is itself a lattice of cells changing state one cell at a time. The ontology contains no particles — only waves that deliver their energy in quanta.

Quantum field theory replaced particles by fields but multiplied the fields, one per species, glued together by hand. The viewpoint here is the opposite one, elementary in computer science: a cellular automaton — a lattice of cells, each holding a finite packet of information, all updated in parallel by one local rule. If the world is such an automaton, everything physical is a wave of information over that lattice, and the theory reduces to two questions: what is the information structure of a single cell, and what is the rule that updates it. Causal Clifford Theory (CL10) answers both concretely: the cell carries the $36$-level module of Axiom 1 — the spinor module of $\mathrm{Cl}(10,0)$ and two neutral singlet sectors — and the rule is a product of $\mathrm{Spin}(10)$ plane rotations interleaved with nearest-neighbour swap-rotations.

### 1.2 Two literatures, one construction

Discrete models of quantum dynamics and grand unification have developed independently. Causal sets, lattice gauge theory and quantum cellular automata treat spacetime as a finite, causal, unitary dynamical system [1,2,3,4,5]. The observation that one generation of quarks and leptons fits exactly into the $16$-dimensional spinor of $\mathrm{Spin}(10)$, with the $5/3$ hypercharge normalisation and anomaly cancellation following from the embedding, has been standard since 1974 [6,7,8,9]; Clifford-algebra formulations of this unification exist [10,11,12,13]. 't Hooft's cellular-automaton interpretation supplies a deterministic unitary dynamics but no unification [14,15].

CL10 puts these on a single footing: the internal space of every sub-site of the automaton's cell **is** the $36$-level cell module — the spinor module of $\mathrm{Cl}(10,0)$ with its two neutral singlet sectors (Section 4) — and the update **is** an element of the corresponding spin group, times the singlet ladder phases of Axiom 1. Nothing else is added by hand: no field outside the cell module, no extra gauge factor, no second algebra.

The comparison is decidable, and its deficits are named here rather than left to the Discussion. Against the Clifford-algebra programmes [10,11,12,13] this construction lacks what they already hold: a semiclassical renormalisation group acting on a spectrum of light chiral fermions, and couplings that run into agreement with data. Here no level of the measured spectrum is light, dispersing and chiral at either clock — the question is O10 (Sections 8.2–8.3, 9, 6) — and agreement with the couplings is reached only by admitting two fitted desert thresholds (O4). Against the cellular-automaton and causal-programme literature [1,2,3,4,5,14,15] what is added is the internal symmetry, the exact unitarity of a finite-order update, and a boundary computed rather than asserted: the conical node a light chiral fermion would require lies outside the automaton's own circuit class — $60$ gapless points among $33\,800$ in the largest covariant stage family, every one of them flat or Abelian (Section 8.4). Against both literatures stands the doubling, which is neither ignored nor evaded into an extra dimension [16] but displayed and then gapped by a factor of the root's own algebra, at the measured price of replacing the light cone with a Lieb–Robinson bound [17].

## 2. The root: the algebra, the theorem, and the step

**Axiom 1 (the root).** *The universe is a $(1{+}3)$-dimensional causal quantum cellular automaton. Its cell is a $2{\times}2{\times}2$ block of Planck sites — site spacing $\ell_P$, so the cells tile a cubic lattice of spacing $2\ell_P$ — and each of the eight sub-sites carries the cell module*

$$M = S \oplus R_A \oplus R_B, \qquad \dim M = 36,$$

*where $S$ is the $32$-dimensional Dirac module of the Clifford algebra $\mathrm{Cl}(10,0)$ — ten mutually anticommuting Hermitian generators*

$$h_a^\dagger = h_a,\qquad h_a^2 = I,\qquad \{h_a,h_b\} = 0 \quad (a\ne b),\qquad a,b = 1,\dots,10,$$

*— and $R_A$, $R_B$ are two neutral singlet sectors: each is a conjugate pair of levels $\{A_\pm\}$, $\{B_\pm\}$; the generators act on them trivially, equivalently every one of the $45$ plane generators $\tfrac14[h_a,h_b]$ acts as zero (Section 4). A singlet of the full Clifford algebra is not chiral, so its levels come in conjugate pairs. A $\mathbb{Z}_2$, the **B-parity**, is odd on $B$ alone. The state of the universe is a tensor product of these modules over the sub-sites, so the cell's on-site space is $\mathbb{C}^{288}$. The hypothesis of everything proved below is this algebra and this module — nothing else: the time evolution is a separate construction (**Construction 1**), and no property of it enters the proofs.*

Three structural facts about the root, all standard [10,34]:

1. $\mathrm{Cl}(10,0)$ is $2^{10} = 1024$-dimensional; its complexification is $M_{32}(\mathbb{C})$, so it has a single irreducible complex module of dimension $32$, the Dirac module.
2. The volume element $w = h_1 h_2 \cdots h_{10}$ satisfies
   $$w^2 = (-1)^{10\cdot 9/2}\prod_a h_a^2 = (-1)^{45} = -I,\qquad w^\dagger = -w,$$
   so $w$ is anti-Hermitian with eigenvalues $\pm i$, each of multiplicity $16$. The projectors $P_{16} = \tfrac12(I - i w)$ and $P_{\overline{16}} = \tfrac12(I + i w)$ split the Dirac module into two inequivalent $16$-dimensional Weyl modules.
3. The algebra is $\mathbb{Z}_2$-graded by the parity of the number of generators in a product: $512$ even and $512$ odd elements.

**Main Theorem (the content of the root).** *Let $\mathrm{Cl}(10,0)$ be the algebra of Axiom 1 and $M = S \oplus R_A \oplus R_B$ its cell module. From the algebra and the module alone — no evolution, no lattice spacing, no coupling, no measurement — the following are forced:*

*(i) a $(1{+}3)$ spacetime subalgebra whose quadratic form is Minkowski, with its null cone (Theorem 1, Corollary 1);*

*(ii) the split of $S$ into two inequivalent $16$-dimensional Weyl modules $P_{16}$, $P_{\overline{16}}$, and the $\mathbf{16}$ is exactly the six left-handed Weyl fields of one Standard Model generation (structural fact 2, Proposition 1);*

*(iii) the gauge content: the $45$ plane generators close to $\mathfrak{so}(10)$ and contain the Model algebra, and the hypercharge on the $\mathbf 5$ obeys $\mathrm{Tr}\,Y^2 = \tfrac56$ — the $5/3$ normalisation (Theorem 3);*

*(iv) anomaly freedom: the eight gauge and mixed anomaly coefficients of the $\mathbf{16}$ vanish (Theorem 2, Table 2);*

*(v) statistics: the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ grading of the algebra is the grading that carries spin and statistics on the module, and the B-parity is a grading of the cell (Section 6);*

*(vi) the content is one generation: $\mathrm{Cl}_{10}(\mathbb{C}) \simeq M_{32}(\mathbb{C})$ has a single irreducible complex module (structural fact 1), and $10$ is the smallest dimension with properties (ii) and (iii) ("Why $10$", below).*

*Nothing in (i)-(vi) mentions time evolution; no measured property of the automaton's step is used in their proofs.*

**Construction 1 (the step).** *The dynamics is one time-independent unitary **step** of six **stages** built from the same algebra (Section 7.1): each stage applies the internal layer to every sub-site — on $S$ the product of the $45$ plane rotations, and on the two singlet pairs the phases $e^{\pm i\,\mathrm{loop}^3}$ and $e^{\pm i\,\mathrm{loop}^{10}}$, with $\mathrm{loop} = 1/16\pi$: a level on ladder rung $n$ ([Paper III Section 2.4]) carries the phase $m/M_P$ per elementary time, its mass read as a phase. The elementary time is the stage, $t_P$; one step is $6t_P$.*

**Why the cell is a block.** The bond part of the update pairs neighbouring sub-sites, and the pairing that treats the three directions alike has six stages: three pair sub-sites *inside* the cell, three pair sub-sites *across* cells. Written on a single Planck site the same rule alternates between bonds inside and outside the site, which forces a two-cell spatial period and a six-periodic time dependence; on the block it is one time-independent rule, and the block is the smallest region on which the update acts as a single unitary. The staggering is therefore not a time dependence of the law but the sub-lattice structure of the cell, and the Bloch decomposition of the step is exact at every step.

The step's own properties are separate claims, booked where they are measured: exact unitarity and $\det U = 1$ and the causal radius (Theorem 5, Section 7), the $\pm$-spectral mirror (Theorem 4, Section 7.2), the doubling census and its Wilson gapping (Proposition 2, Section 8), the closure of the clock to $g = \pi/4$ (Theorem 6, Section 9), and the empirical contacts of the rung phases ([Paper III Section 2]). None of them is a consequence of the Main Theorem, and none of them is needed by it.

**Why $10$.** The number $10$ is the smallest $n$ for which a single complex Clifford algebra simultaneously provides (i) a pseudoscalar splitting a Dirac module into two *inequivalent* Weyl modules of dimension $16$ — at $n = 8$ the Weyl modules are $8$-dimensional ($\mathrm{Spin}(8)$ triality: $8_v, 8_s, 8_c$; the $16$-dimensional representations are reducible) — and (ii) an adjoint of dimension $\binom{10}{2} = 45$ large enough to contain $\mathfrak{su}(5)$ and hence the Standard Model algebra. At $n = 9$, $\mathrm{Cl}_9(\mathbb{C}) \simeq M_{16}\oplus M_{16}$ does have $16$-dimensional irreducibles, but they are the two simple factors of the algebra rather than a pseudoscalar split, and $\mathfrak{so}(9)$ does not contain $\mathfrak{su}(5)$. The root is therefore selected by the requirement of a single chiral generation with a unified gauge algebra, not chosen.

## 3. Spacetime as a subalgebra

**Theorem 1.** *Let $T = h_1$ and $S_a = i h_{a+1}$ for $a = 1,2,3$. Then*
$$\begin{gathered}
T^2 = +I,\qquad S_a^2 = -I,\\
\{T,S_a\} = 0,\qquad \{S_a,S_b\} = 0 .
\end{gathered}$$
*The real span of $\{T, S_1, S_2, S_3\}$ inside the grade-1 part of the algebra, with the quadratic form induced by the Clifford product, is Minkowski space $\mathbb{R}^{1,3}$.*

*Proof.* The four generators $h_1,\dots,h_4$ anticommute by Axiom 1. Multiplication by $i$ turns $h_a^2 = I$ into $(i h_a)^2 = -I$ and preserves anticommutation. For $v = v_0 T + \sum_a v_a S_a$ with real coefficients, $v^2 = (v_0^2 - \sum_a v_a^2)I$.

**Corollary 1 (the light cone).** *A vector $v$ in that real span is null, $v^2 = 0$, if and only if $v_0^2 = \sum_a v_a^2$. The null cone of this quadratic form is the light cone of the lattice.*

Two qualifications. The null cone is a **cone in the grade-1 part**, not a subalgebra: it is not closed under the Clifford product, since $(T+S_1)(T-S_1) = 2I - 2TS_1$ is a grade-0 plus grade-2 element. And the choice of *which* four of the ten generators play the role of spacetime is a choice of frame in the $10$-dimensional generator space, not a prediction: the algebra supplies a family of equivalent $(1{+}3)$ subalgebras related by orthogonal transformations of the generator space. The remaining six generators are inert under the Lorentz generators $\Sigma_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac14[\Gamma_\mu,\Gamma_\nu]$ built from $\Gamma_\mu \in \{T,S_a\}$.

**Causality is a postulate with a dynamical content.** The light cone by itself does not bound signal propagation: a unitary on-site rotation alone has infinite propagation speed. The bound is supplied by the update of Section 7, where the causal radius is *implemented* by the bond stages and *measured* to be exactly one Planck cell per stage (Theorem 5). The light cone and the causal radius are distinct statements, and only the second is a property of the dynamics.

## 4. Matter: the 16 Weyl spinor

**Proposition 1.** *Under $\mathrm{SU}(3)_c\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$ the $16$-dimensional Weyl module is the six left-handed fields of one Standard Model generation:*

$$16 = Q(\mathbf{3},\mathbf{2},+\tfrac16) \oplus u^c(\bar{\mathbf{3}},\mathbf{1},-\tfrac23) \oplus d^c(\bar{\mathbf{3}},\mathbf{1},+\tfrac13) \oplus L(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2},-\tfrac12) \oplus e^c(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1},+1) \oplus \nu^c(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1},0),$$

*with $6+3+3+2+1+1 = 16$. Under $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ this is $\mathbf{10}\oplus\bar{\mathbf{5}}\oplus\mathbf{1}$, the $\mathbf{1}$ being the sterile $\nu^c$.*

The assignment is the standard one [6,7,8,9,12]. What the automaton adds is that the $\mathbf{16}$ is not put in by hand but is the Weyl eigenspace of the pseudoscalar of the same algebra that carries the gauge group and the spacetime — and, as [Paper II Section 3.8] measures (V211), neither is the group a free choice: the stabiliser, inside the $45$, of the four directions the automaton calls spacetime is $\mathrm{Spin}(4)\times\mathrm{Spin}(6)$, with $\mathfrak{su}(3)\oplus\mathfrak u(1)$ inside $\mathrm{spin}(6)$, and the $B-L$ charges of the previous paragraph and the $5/3$ normalisation both follow as trace ratios. What the construction leaves open is the flavour structure of [Paper III Section 3], not the group.

**$B-L$.** $B-L$ is a generator of $\mathfrak{so}(10)$, so it is *not* a single number on the $\mathbf{16}$. Its values on the six fields are $Q: \tfrac13$, $u^c: -\tfrac13$, $d^c: -\tfrac13$, $L: -1$, $e^c: +1$, $\nu^c: +1$; the $\overline{\mathbf{16}}$ carries the negatives of all of them. Under $\mathrm{Spin}(10) \supset \mathrm{SU}(5)\times \mathrm{U}(1)_\chi$, $16 \to \mathbf{10}_{-1}\oplus\bar{\mathbf{5}}_{+2}\oplus\mathbf{1}_{+3}$, and $B-L$ is a linear combination of $\chi$ and the diagonal $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ generator, not $\chi$ itself.

**Theorem 2 (anomaly cancellation).** *All eight gauge and mixed anomaly coefficients of the $\mathbf{16}$ vanish.*

## 7. The step: unitarity, spectrum, causality

### 7.1 The update

The step has six stages, and every stage is the same two layers: the internal rotation applied to every sub-site, then one bond stage. The internal factor is built from the $\mathrm{Spin}(10)$ plane-rotation generators $T_{ab} = i\,h_a h_b$ ($a<b$) in the $32$-dimensional module — each $T_{ab}$ Hermitian, squaring to $I$, traceless (sixteen $+1$ and sixteen $-1$ eigenvalues):

$$U(g) \;=\; \prod_{a<b}\Big(\cos g\, I - i \sin g\, T_{ab}\Big).$$

A sub-site is labelled by $e = (e_1,e_2,e_3) \in \{0,1\}^3$, the bit of axis $j$ being $2^j$. A **bond stage** acts on the four disjoint pairs $(e,\, e \oplus 2^j)$: an *intra-block* stage takes the pairs with $e_j = 0$, inside the cell; an *inter-block* stage takes the pairs joining $e_j = 1$ of one cell to $e_j = 0$ of the next cell along $\hat e_j$ — still a two-cell operator, now between neighbouring cells. On each pair the gate is the swap-rotation $\cos g\, I - i\sin g\,(|e\rangle\langle o| + |o\rangle\langle e|)$, carrying the same single angle and no other parameter. The six stages run in the fixed order (intra $x$, inter $y$, intra $z$, inter $x$, intra $y$, inter $z$). One step is therefore

$$M \;=\; \prod_{s=1}^{6} \big(P_s \otimes U(g)\big),$$

an operator on the cell's on-site space $\mathbb{C}^{288}$ ($P_s$ on the eight sub-sites, $U$ on the $36$-dimensional cell module: the $32$-dimensional spinor block above, and on the singlet pairs the ladder phases $e^{\pm i\,\mathrm{loop}^3}$, $e^{\pm i\,\mathrm{loop}^{10}}$ of Axiom 1). At block crystal momentum $q$ an inter-block pair carries the phase $e^{\pm iq}$, $M(q)$ is $288 \times 288$, and on the $16$ sector it is $128 \times 128$.

Every spinor factor is special unitary: the $45$ internal factors have $\det = \exp(-i g\,\mathrm{Tr}\,T_{ab}) = 1$ on $S$, and each bond pair block $\left(\begin{smallmatrix} c & -is\\ -is & c\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ has determinant $c^2 + s^2 = 1$ (the inter-block phases cancel). Hence

$$U U^\dagger = I,\quad \det U = 1, \qquad M M^\dagger = I,\quad \det M = 1 \qquad \text{for every } g,$$

on the spinor sector, and on the full cell as well: the four singlet levels come in conjugate pairs $e^{\pm i\varphi}$, so their phases multiply to $1$,

$$\det \tilde U = 1, \qquad \det \tilde M = 1, \qquad \text{and} \qquad \sum_k \omega_k = \arg\det \tilde U = 0 \;\text{ on all } 36 \text{ levels}:$$

the $\pm$-pairing, the unit determinant and the vanishing $0$-point hold on the whole cell, singlets included, and the cell step is exactly unitary with the B-parity an exact symmetry of it (the parity commutator vanishes to machine precision, Section 4). Verified numerically: internal unitarity at $1.3\times10^{-15}$ with $|\det U - 1| = 1.9\times10^{-14}$, and for the full cell matrix $\|MM^\dagger - I\| \le 9.1\times10^{-15}$ and $|\det M - 1| \le 4.0\times10^{-13}$ at the zone centre, at a generic momentum and at the two high-symmetry points — all in V196, the $M(q)$ sweep in its T5 row; the real-space six-stage step preserves the norm of a random cell state to $10^{-15}$. Two structural inputs must be named: the $45$ factors do not commute, so $U$ depends on the *ordering* of the product, and the six stages have a fixed order. The algebra does not select either order.

The angle $g$ is the one continuous number of the dynamics, and the closure axiom removes it as a choice: only $g = \pi/2$ and $g = \pi/4$ make the step an element of the root (Theorem 6, Section 9), the half-angle branch degenerates to a pure translation, and the physical clock is the order-$8$ root $g = \pi/4$. ### 7.3 Locality and the causal radius

**Theorem 5 (the causal radius is implemented).** *On an $(L_b,L_b,L_b)$ torus of cells the step is a product of two-cell operators: every bond stage couples sub-sites at Planck-cell distance $\le 1$ — an intra-block pair joins neighbouring sub-sites of one cell, an inter-block pair joins neighbouring cells. Hence after $n$ stages the support of a sub-site-localised excitation lies inside the $n$-cell light cone: at most one Planck cell per stage, and since three of the six stages are inter-block, at most three blocks — six Planck cells — per step. Numerically: the norm of a random cell state is preserved to $10^{-15}$; on an $8^3$-cell torus the support radius after $n$ stages is exactly $n$ Planck cells for every $n \le 12$ — the front saturates the cone, front speed exactly $1$; on a $32^3$-cell torus run for $30$ stages the front reaches $21$ cells, and no site of the support and no amplitude ever appears outside the $n$-cell cone ($0$, to machine precision); the same cone is realised at $g = 0.3$ as at $1/5$; the internal layer alone, with the bond stages removed, keeps the excitation on one sub-site forever.*

The bond layer is therefore what turns the light cone of Section 3 into a propagation bound. This is the cleanest dynamical result of the theory.

## 8. Lattice chirality and the doubling problem

The Nielsen–Ninomiya theorem forbids a single chiral fermion in a theory with a *local, translation-invariant, Hermitian* lattice Dirac operator and a bounded spectrum [24]. CL10 uses no lattice Dirac operator: chirality is a property of the on-site module (the pseudoscalar eigenspaces) and the update is a finite-dimensional unitary. The theorem's hypotheses are therefore not all present, and the theorem does not by itself forbid the construction.

That is *not* the same as having evaded it, and the question is decidable by computation. The step is one time-independent operator on the cell lattice, so the good quantum number is the **block crystal momentum** $q$ — the phase under a translation by one automaton cell, $2\pi$-periodic, zone $q \in [-\pi,\pi]^3$ — and the eight sub-sites are the internal structure of the Bloch matrix. The $16$-sector one-step matrix $M(q)$ is computed exactly at $128 \times 128$ and cross-checked against the real-space step on a $2^3$-block torus (agreement $10^{-14}$). The cubic high-symmetry momenta carry the whole spectrum: $\Gamma = (0,0,0)$; $\mathrm{X} = (\pm\tfrac\pi2,0,0)$, star $6$; $\mathrm{M} = (\pm\tfrac\pi2,\pm\tfrac\pi2,0)$, star $12$, little group $C_{2v}$; $\mathrm{R} = (\pm\tfrac\pi2,\pm\tfrac\pi2,\pm\tfrac\pi2)$, star $8$, little group $C_{3v}$.

### 8.2 Lorentz structure at the nodes

The causal front speed of Theorem 5 is exactly $1$ Planck cell per stage; that is the statement the automaton proves. What the *low-energy spectrum* of the implemented step does not have is continuous Lorentz invariance.

On the $g = 1/5$ member of the Wilson family, which carries the dispersive node spectrum (on the root clock the node spectra are pinned to $\{0,\pi\}$, Proposition 2, and the dispersion lives on the nodal lines, Section 8.3): at all twelve nodes and in all $1200$ sampled directions the dispersion is **linear** (local exponent $p = 1.00$, no quadratic band touching), but the cone is strongly **anisotropic**: principal velocities $(0.200, 0.200, 0.284)$ per unit block momentum — $(0.100, 0.100, 0.142)$ in Planck-cell units — anisotropy $0.748$–$0.758$, the same at every node of the $O_h$ orbit. The lattice signature is universal, not a defect. Exhaustive scanning of the whole Wilson family closes the hope that isotropy emerges somewhere: the node gap is located at every zero over the two orbit representatives $\times$ $60$ angles ($2726$ gapless points, tolerance $10^{-7}$) and the cone anisotropy $A = (v_{\max}-v_{\min})/(v_{\max}+v_{\min})$ measured over the $179$ sphere directions at each point (V115); the Lorentz target $A < 10^{-6}$ contains $0$ of the $2726$ points, the closest approach being $A = 0.997$.

Frame averaging over the internal frames is blind to the failure, and this closes the last proposed escape of this kind: rebuilding the internal step in four random $\mathrm{SO}(10)$ frames of the ten generators (the product of the $45$ plane rotations taken in the rotated frame's lexicographic order) leaves, on the $g = 1/5$ member of the Wilson family used as the reference spectrum, the node gap $2.003\times10^{-4}$, the principal velocities $(0.200, 0.200, 0.284)$ and the anisotropy $0.7545$ unchanged to $3\times10^{-15}$, and the tangent commutators at the noise floor ($\le 1\times10^{-11}$) in every frame. The momentum dependence lives entirely in the sub-site factor of the Kronecker bond layer, so no choice or averaging of the internal frame can move the cone. The anisotropy is now a measured experimental contact: read against the electron-sector SME velocity ceiling $\delta v/v \le 8.0\times10^{-15}$ (Kostelecký–Russell data tables, Table D6, $|c^{(TY)}_{ee}|$ [38]), the measured tensor gives $\delta v/v = 0.296$ at the implemented clock, $1$ at the closed clock (one axis exactly flat), $0.295$ on the selective-reading Wilson cones at $\theta^* = 1.730784$, and $0.9999$ on the star nodal line — the best of the named set, $0.292$, is $3.7\times10^{13}$ times the ceiling: **F9 fires**, fourteen orders of magnitude past the bound at the most isotropic named node (V235). The dressed electric gate does not move it at all: at the closed clock the gate is a scalar ($5\pi/4$, err $3\times10^{-14}$) and the step's six bond factors make the six node insertions multiply to $+I$, so the dressed spectrum is identical to the bare one and the dressed network's gapless points keep $\delta v/v = 1$; the star module reaches $0.9999$ at its line (V236).

### 8.3 The massless-mode census

On the grid the reference member $g = 1/5$ of the Wilson family seems to have **no exactly gapless mode anywhere**: the smallest quasi-energy over a $15^3$ zone grid is $5.1\times10^{-4}$ lattice units, and at the twelve M nodes it is $2.003\times10^{-4}$. The grid is too coarse. Refined inside the three nodal planes, that member has an **exactly gapless orbit of $24$ points** — representative $q_0 = (-2.1890561,\,-1.4445961,\,0)$, gap $4.2\times10^{-14}$, the residual falling to the noise floor under refinement while a displacement of $5\times10^{-2}$ in the plane costs $8.8\times10^{-3}$ — with little group of order $2$. The branch is linear in every in-plane direction with $v \in [0.031, 0.219]$ (anisotropy $0.857$), and leaving the plane costs a gap linear in $q_z$ ($5.1\times10^{-4}$ at $q_z = 10^{-1}$): a massless point node confined to the nodal plane, not a line and not a gapped touching. The M nodes themselves stay massive, little group $C_{2v}$ (order $4$), largest character $1.0$ on the near-gapless bond subspace — no $2$-dimensional irrep there either. So the reference spectrum does contain exactly massless excitations at $g = 1/5$, and it still contains no massless spin-2 candidate: a little group of order $2$ has only $1$-dimensional irreps, so the new nodes carry no helicity $\pm1$ or $\pm2$ pair, and the anisotropy of that exactly massless branch is $0.857$, against the $0.748$–$0.758$ of the near-miss M nodes. At the root clock the picture is the one of Proposition 2 — a pinned zone centre and a gapless line network, no isolated nodes to search for.

The near-miss is the signature of the irrational internal clock, not a structure: at the closed angle $g = \pi/4$ the twelve nodes are exactly gapless (gaps $0$–$2.2\times10^{-16}$, $12/12$) and the zone-centre step is an exact involution — and the census there finds $\Gamma$ plus **six gapless face-diagonal lines** carrying those nodes, with the per-node chirality convention inapplicable and the helicity answer unchanged (Section 9).

The same census settles the spin question at the zone centre. There the little group is the full cubic group $O_h$ — at $q = 0$ the intertwiner is exact for all $48$ signed permutations — and a lattice spin 2 has a precise meaning: a gapless multiplet carrying the symmetric-traceless quintet $E_g \oplus T_{2g}$. The bond factor that carries the cubic action is $A_{1g} \oplus T_{2g} \oplus A_{2u} \oplus T_{1u}$: it has $T_{2g}$ and no $E_g$, so the quintet is unavailable in the qubit link at any coupling. $\Gamma$ is exactly gapless at $g = \pi/8, \pi/6, \pi/4, \pi/3$ and nowhere near the implemented $1/5$ (gapped by $3.65\times10^{-2}$); at each of those angles its null space is cubic-covariant and $E_g$-free — $16 \times (A_{1g} \oplus T_{2g})$, dimension $64$, at $\pi/4$ — and its dispersion is flat, $\omega/|\mathbf{q}| \to 0$. The symmetric rank-2 space of the link directions does carry $A_{1g} \oplus E_g \oplus T_{2g}$: the quintet lives in the directional bond layer of [Paper II Section 2], not in the qubit link ([Paper II Section 3.1]).

The route is built: the directional bond layer, its dressed gate and the quintet dispersion are constructed and computed in [Paper II Section 2.4].

### 8.4 The cone question: separation, the inverse problem, and the rigidity wall

Three proposed routes to a cone at the *implemented* step fail (Section 8.2): the Wilson family, frame averaging, and two hand-built bond mechanisms leave the tangent algebra commuting and the anisotropy pinned. What the absence of a cone in a finite set of tried mechanisms does not say is whether a cone exists *somewhere* in the space of mechanisms. This subsection makes the question precise and answers it for the whole space: three results, each a proposition.

**The tangent algebra, measured.** Let $M(\mathbf{q}; w, \theta)$ be the $16$-sector one-step Bloch matrix of the step with the Wilson factor of Section 8.1, and $k \in \{x, y, z\}$. The (unprojected) tangent operator in direction $k$ is

$$T_k \;=\; i\,(\partial_k M)\,M^\dagger\,,$$

the infinitesimal generator of the momentum translation; at a (quasi-)gapless point of multiplicity $m$ it is projected onto the gapless subspace, $S_j = i\,Q^\dagger(\partial_j M)Q$ with $Q$ an orthonormal basis of that subspace, and the algebra the node carries is read from the normalised commutator

$$\rho_{\mathrm{comm}} \;=\; \frac{\max_{a<b}\,\lVert S_a S_b - S_b S_a\rVert}{\tfrac13\sum_j \lVert S_j\rVert^2}\,,$$

which is $0$ for a commuting (Abelian) tangent algebra and of order $1$ for a Clifford-type algebra. A gapless point is **conical** when $\rho_{\mathrm{comm}} > 0.5$ and the cone is isotropic ($A < 0.1$ in the notation of Section 8.2).

**Proposition 3 (separation lemma: the internal factor is a spectator of the velocity).** *(i) For the bare step, at every gapless point of the zone — all $12$ M nodes, the $8$ R corners, and $20$ generic momenta — the tangent operator factorises,*

$$T_k \;=\; A_k(\mathbf{q}) \otimes I_{16}\,,$$

*with Kronecker defect at the noise floor ($4.5\times10^{-16}$; the internal off-diagonal content of $T_k$ is $2.9\times10^{-16}$). The $\mathbf{16}$ is not only closed by the step (Proposition 2): its momentum derivative cannot mix the internal factor — the internal module is blind to the momentum. *(ii) The Wilson factor adds no velocity structure:* with $(w, \theta) = (0.70, 1.10)$ the tangent operator decomposes as a transport term $\otimes I_{16}$ plus an internal term that is diagonal in the unification Cartan directions of [Paper III Section 3.1] (off-diagonal content $2.9\times10^{-16}$, and after the predicted Cartan term is removed a $\otimes I_{16}$ defect of $4.5\times10^{-16}$), and at the gapless corner triplet the Cartan coefficient is the same on the three axis directions: the Wilson factor is one common internal-diagonal phase, which can shift levels but cannot rotate the tangent algebra — the reason the commutators of the gapless corner of Section 8.2 sit at the noise floor. *Consequence:* the only freedom left that can build a non-commuting tangent algebra is the bond layer.

**Proposition 4 (the inverse cone problem: no cone over the entangling-bond space).** The remaining freedom is the bond layer, and it is searched rather than sampled. The space: $220$ axis-covariant entangling bond configurations — the full index set of $120$ cyclic $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ triplets $(T_{ab}, T_{bc}, T_{ca})$ of the $45$ plane rotations, $60$ disjoint commuting triples $(T_{ab}, T_{cd}, T_{ef})$, and $40$ random even involutions $D = \mathrm{sign}(H)$ of a random real combination of $12$ plane rotations — each in four $(w, \theta)$ patterns (two bond strengths, two sign conventions), $880$ tuned points in total, each tuned over the $(w, \theta)$ plane to the point of minimum corner-triplet gap (a coarse $12\times19$ grid, then four golden-section rounds per variable, then a $150$-evaluation Nelder–Mead polish; the $20$ best candidates refined further, one bond fully optimised). No point of the programme satisfies the cone criterion. The best gap found anywhere is the implemented step's own corner triplet ($1.5\times10^{-16}$ top, $5.4\times10^{-16}$ spread); the largest $\rho_{\mathrm{comm}}$ reached by any non-trivial bond at its best-gap point is $0.362$; the fully optimised bond lands at gap $3.7\times10^{-4}$ with $\rho_{\mathrm{comm}} = 0.023$ and an anticommutator $1.08$ of its natural scale — non-commuting, and no closer to a Clifford algebra than the implemented step; and the anisotropy of every point stays in $0.9947$–$1$. The M-node doubler survives the best entangling bond (node gap $1.9\times10^{-18}$ against $8.8\times10^{-21}$ for the implemented step): the bond layer does not remove the doubling either. The causal accounting closes with the search: the degree-$1$ Fourier leakage ($0.27$ for the three-axis bond of Section 8.2) is the Wilson Bessel tail, not the bond — measured $2.7\times10^{-1}$ for the entangling and for the implemented bond at the same $w$, and $8.7\times10^{-17}$ for the entangling bond at zero Wilson strength. The entangling bond costs nothing of the causal radius of Theorem 5, and it still does not build a cone.

**Proposition 5 (the rigidity wall).** A finite search excluding a point is a claim about a space, and the space is measured. Around the $12$ tuned bonds of largest $\rho_{\mathrm{comm}}$, $5292$ points of the $(w, \theta)$ plane are recorded as the pair $(\mathrm{gap}, \rho_{\mathrm{comm}})$, and the **frontier**

$$g_{\min}(\rho) \;=\; \min\big\{\,\mathrm{gap} \;:\; \rho_{\mathrm{comm}} > \rho\,\big\}$$

is computed from them (the programme runs on the implemented clock $g = 1/5$, the clock of the bond space it quantifies; each experiment pins that clock in its own `CL10_ANGLE` before `cl10` is imported, since the configuration freezes the clock at import time). Four results. *(i) Non-commuting algebras exist in the space:* the largest $\rho_{\mathrm{comm}}$ found is $1.033$ (the $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-triplet bond on the index triple $(5, 8, 9)$), a fully non-commuting tangent algebra — $1.7\times$ beyond the best mechanism of Section 8.2 and $2.9\times$ beyond the best-gap bound of the scan, which measured commutators only at each bond's most-gapless point. *(ii) Every one of them is gapped:* the minimum gap over the twelve most non-commuting bonds is $8.0\times10^{-3}$, and no one of the $5292$ points has $\mathrm{gap} < 10^{-8}$ with $\rho_{\mathrm{comm}} > 0.5$. *(iii) The frontier is monotone and floored:* $g_{\min}(\rho) = 6.6\times10^{-3}$ at $\rho = 0.05$ and $\rho = 0.10$, and $g_{\min}(\rho) = 8.0\times10^{-3}$ for every $\rho \in [0.2, 0.5]$ — however non-commuting the requested tangent algebra is, the gap cannot be driven below $8.0\times10^{-3}$. *(iv) The anchor:* the implemented bond at its exactly gapless corner (gap $2.7\times10^{-16}$) has $\rho_{\mathrm{comm}} = 6.7\times10^{-12}$ — maximally Abelian. The two quantities are not related by a power law either: the log–log slope of $\rho_{\mathrm{comm}}$ against the gap along $25$-point rays from each tuned bond toward the gapless anchor has median $-0.28$ ($4$ of $12$ positive). The separation between the two regimes is a **wall** at gap $\simeq 8.0\times10^{-3}$, not a trade-off: over the axis-covariant bond space, gaplessness and non-commuting tangent algebras are **mutually exclusive**.

**Proposition 6 (the parity no-go, and the cone outside the class).** *The flatness of Section 8.2 and the wall of Proposition 5 have one cause, and it is representation theory. Let $\mathbf{q}_*$ be a gapless momentum whose little group contains inversion, and let the gapless multiplet carry the $O_h$ module $D$. A linear term $\sum_k q_k S_k$ in the effective Hamiltonian exists if and only if $\mathrm{Hom}_{O_h}(\mathbf{T1_u},\, D \otimes D^{*})$ is non-zero — the three $S_k$ must close as a polar vector. But $D \otimes D^{*}$ is even under inversion for every $D$, while $\mathbf{q}$ is odd. The multiplicity is $0$ for all ten irreps, measured by the character inner product and again by direct linear algebra on the $\mathbf{T1_u}$ block of the implemented bond factor (solution space of dimension $0$, smallest singular value $9.8$). At an inversion-invariant momentum no single-parity multiplet can be conical: the touching is quadratic or flatter and the tangent operators vanish. That is the $\omega/|\mathbf{q}| \to 0$ of the $\Gamma$ scans of Section 8.3, and it is why its exactly gapless zone-centre null space $16 \times (A_{1g} \oplus T_{2g})$ — two even modules — is flat by necessity and not by tuning.*

*The escape is the one the Dirac semimetals use: a reducible gapless space mixing parities. The bond factor is $A_{1g} \oplus T_{2g} \oplus A_{2u} \oplus T_{1u}$ (Section 8.3), and the products $A_{1g} \otimes T_{1u} \supset \mathbf{T1_u}$ and $T_{1u} \otimes T_{2g} \supset \mathbf{T1_u}$ single out the two opposite-parity pairs as the only ones a linear term can couple. Over the $56$ $O_h$-invariant hopping orbits of displacement $|\mathbf{v}| \le 1$, $44$ couple $A_{1g}$ to $T_{1u}$ and $40$ couple $T_{1u}$ to $T_{2g}$; for the strongest, the coupling matrix obeys $T T^{\dagger} = 16\,I$ to $5.6\times10^{-16}$ — isotropy forced by Schur, not fitted. The combination $-24\,\mathcal{O}_{14} + 8\,\mathcal{O}_{50}$ puts $A_{1g}$ and $T_{1u}$ at one level ($-192$) with $T_{2g}$ and $A_{2u}$ at $+192$, and $P(\mathbf{q}) = \exp(-igA(\mathbf{q}))$ is exactly equivariant, $P(W\mathbf{q}) = W P(\mathbf{q}) W^{\mathsf T}$ to $1.2\times10^{-14}$ over all $48$ elements. With the internal angle tuned so that one internal eigenphase cancels the common phase, the step is gapless at $\Gamma$ to $3\times10^{-11}$ on a $4$-dimensional multiplet $A_{1g} \oplus T_{1u}$, with cone anisotropy $A = 1.0\times10^{-15}$ over $215$ directions, $\rho_{\mathrm{comm}} = 0.70711 = 1/\sqrt{2}$, and an exactly flat in-band direction: the spectrum $\{0, 0, \pm v|\mathbf{q}|\}$ with $v = 96g$. By the criterion of this section the point is conical.*

*What it costs is the rule, not the symmetry at the node. The six-stage product is exactly equivariant at $\Gamma$ — $W P(\mathbf{q}) W^{\mathsf T} = P(R\mathbf{q})$ to $1.1\times10^{-16}$, which is the statement Section 8.3 uses — and breaks $O_h$ at every other momentum: $1.07\times10^{-1}$ at generic $\mathbf{q}$, $1.40\times10^{-1}$ at the M nodes, $1.46\times10^{-1}$ at the R corners, against $1.1\times10^{-15}$ for its stage sum at all momenta. So the obstacle at $\Gamma$ is spectral: at the single angle the root uses the four bond levels are $+6g, -2g, -6g, +2g$, and the $A_{1g}$ and $T_{1u}$ levels are neither coincident nor half a turn apart. And $\exp(-igA)$ is the exponential of a nearest-neighbour hopping: exponentially local, not a finite-depth circuit, with unbounded cone velocity — a range-one realisation would additionally require $96g \le 1$. The cone of this proposition is therefore not a node of the automaton. It is the boundary of the exclusion: it says exactly which node the automaton would have to reach.*

**What this settles, and what remains.** The three propositions close the cone question of this paper in the direction of the no-cone verdict of Section 8.2, and convert it from a search result into a statement about the space: the momentum derivative of the bare step is internal-blind (Proposition 3(i)), the Wilson factor is a spectator of the tangent algebra (Proposition 3(ii)), no axis-covariant entangling bond builds a cone while paying nothing of the causal radius (Proposition 4), and the exclusion is structural — the gapless region of the bond space is Abelian to $10^{-11}$, the non-commuting region is gapped by at least $8.0\times10^{-3}$, and the wall between them is not a scaling (Proposition 5). The nodal-plane structure of Section 8.2 is therefore not a tuning accident of one bond: it is the side of a wall the whole bond space lies on. Proposition 6 settles the residue left open in this section, in both directions. No cone exists at an inversion-invariant momentum on a single-parity multiplet — the flatness of Section 8.3 is a theorem of $O_h$, not a search failure — and outside the axis-covariant class a cone does exist: gapless to $3\times10^{-11}$, isotropic to $10^{-15}$, $\rho_{\mathrm{comm}} = 1/\sqrt{2}$, on a parity-degenerate $A_{1g} \oplus T_{1u}$ pair. What remains open is whether the root's own six-stage product can be brought to that node, and the two-angle search reported here answers it negatively, for the reason Proposition 6 gives. The family searched is the axis-symmetric two-angle one — one angle on the three intra-block stages, one on the three inter-block ones — the largest stage family that keeps $\Gamma$ covariant: covariant there to $1.1\times10^{-16}$, while a generic six-angle product misses by $6.0\times10^{-2}$ (best of $200$ draws) and a per-axis generalisation by $0.90$. Searched jointly with the internal angle — $33\,800$ points of $(a, b, \theta)$, then polished to residuals below $4.3\times10^{-14}$ — $60$ points carry on-site states at phase exactly zero, the gap of the full $128\times128$ step being at most $4.4\times10^{-15}$. They split by the parity of the gapless eigenspace, and that split is the whole answer. $8$ of them have a $4$-dimensional gapless space of a single parity, $A_{1g} \oplus T_{2g}$ or $A_{2u} \oplus T_{1u}$, the content read off the eigenspace by character inner product rather than assumed; Proposition 6 forbids the linear term there, and the measurement obliges — the largest slope over the $179$ sampled directions is $1.3\times10^{-12}$, a flat band. The other $52$ have an $8$-dimensional parity-mixed gapless space, $A_{1g} \oplus T_{2g} \oplus A_{2u} \oplus T_{1u}$: there the linear term is allowed and the touching does disperse, slope $0.09$–$1.73$ per unit block momentum, which stays inside the causal bound ($0.03$–$0.58$ Planck cells per stage against the measured front speed $0.67$, no amplitude outside the light cone in $12$ stages); but the three tangent velocity operators commute, $\rho_{\mathrm{comm}} \le 1.2\times10^{-11}$, at the same anisotropy $0.423$ on every one of the $52$ — the Section 8.2 nodal-plane structure, not a cone. So the automaton's own circuit reproduces both sides of the wall and crosses neither: the multiplets it can make gapless at $\Gamma$ are exactly the ones the parity no-go holds flat, and the parity-mixed ones it can make disperse are exactly the ones whose tangent algebra commutes. Conical nodes of the causal six-stage product: $0$. On the diagonal $a = b$ the same measurement at $\pi/4$ and $3\pi/4$ finds $64$ gapless states carrying $16 \times (A_{1g} \oplus T_{2g})$ — the flat zone-centre band of Section 8.3, gap at most $8\times10^{-17}$, slope at most $3\times10^{-14}$ — and the $A_{1g}$ and $T_{1u}$ bond phases there are *opposite*, not degenerate: the condition that pairs two bond levels through a $\pm$-symmetric internal spectrum is a half-turn between them, not a coincidence, and the internal spectrum supplies the partner of a cancellation at the opposite internal phase, not half a turn away. Two conditions on three angles: the exactly gapless set is a curve in the family, and it is a curve of flat bands and of Abelian touching. What excludes the Proposition 6 node from the root is therefore not the single-angle rule but the circuit class itself.

**The experimental bound that would bite, and why it does not.** An anisotropic gapless mode is precisely the low-energy Lorentz-violating sector the standard-model extension parameterises [38,39]: direction-dependent kinetic terms of the kind the twelve node cones carry are bounded for the dimension-four lepton coefficients at levels running from $10^{-13}$ down to $10^{-21}$ [38]. The automaton is not exposed to that bound as it stands, for a reason measured rather than argued: the Wilson factor re-gaps the twelve node channels, and the split set that survives sits at desert scales, not at $v$. The price of escaping the bound is O10 — the spectrum has no light chiral mode to set against the data. The falsifiable form of the question is therefore conditional, and it is stated in full: *if* a light mode is ever identified inside the measured gapless set, it inherits the anisotropy of [Paper III, Table 2] — velocities $(0.200,\ 0.200,\ 0.284)$, spread $0.75$ — and the standard-model-extension bounds then refute it. The present defence is not a suppression mechanism; it is the absence of a candidate.

### 8.5 The module the no-go points at: the icosahedral star, measured

Proposition 6 puts the obstruction in the little group of the *lattice*, not in the bond layer: wherever the little group of a gapless momentum contains inversion, $\mathrm{Hom}_{O_h}(\mathbf{T1_u},D\otimes D^{*})=0$ kills the linear term whatever the bonds do. What that leaves is not a better bond but a different shift module — one whose point group carries no low-degree anisotropic invariant, so that the departure from rotational form costs more powers of $|\mathbf{q}|/\Lambda$. The census says how many. The implemented module's group is $O_h$, order $48$, Molien counts $[0,1,0,2,0,3,0,4]$: one quadratic invariant, first anisotropic invariant at degree $4$. The icosahedral group, built in the repository from the presentation $[2,3,5]$ (order $60$, generator orders $[2,5]$, product order $3$, element orders $[1,2,3,5]$, presentation trace defect $1.1\times10^{-16}$), has Molien counts $[0,1,0,1,0,2,0,2]$: one quadratic invariant, first anisotropic invariant at degree $6$ (V212). Two more powers of $|\mathbf{q}|/\Lambda$ is the whole prize, and it is bought by changing the module and nothing else: the internal map, the stagger bookkeeping, the clock and the cell sizes stay as committed, and the three cubic axes the engine hops on are replaced by the six directions of the icosahedral star.

**The substitution is exact where it can be, and its price is measured where it cannot.** The star's six directions are six equiangular lines, pairwise cosine $0.447214$ — the same angle spectrum the measured icosahedral group's own axes give — and their fourth moment is isotropic to $2.2\times10^{-16}$, where the three cubic axes are off by $0.400$ (V214). The module has $6$ generators of norm $1.902113$ per step, rank $6$ in three physical dimensions, relation rank $6$, and the relations are exactly the six pairs of opposites; the module is dense in $\mathbb{R}^3$, measured by its shortest element falling from $1.0705$ to $0.2527$ between word bounds $1$ and $5$. Growth and causality stay closed form: the words of at most $k$ steps are the $\ell^1$ ball of $\mathbb{Z}^6$ ($12$, $84$, $376$, $1288$, $3652$, $8988$ enumerated one-to-one against the closed form), and the front after $k$ steps is exactly $k$ times the Lieb–Robinson radius ($11.4127$ at six steps, against $6$ steps of $1.902113$). The price is exact too, and it is the reason the module is a quasilattice rather than a lattice: the cubic axis the engine hops on is *not* a module point (its unique rational representation in the six generators is $[0,0,\tfrac12,\tfrac12,0,0]$), $x+y$ costs two steps, $2x$ is not reachable at all, and the shortest hop along a rational direction is eight steps long. There is therefore no Brillouin zone in the periodic-lattice sense — the module has no rational period lattice — and momentum means over it are almost-periodic box means rather than Fourier projectors.

**One algebraic route to the same symmetry is closed, by a divisibility test.** The finite link group the root generates (the $10$ Clifford involutions) has order $2048$ with element orders $[1,2,4]$: a $2$-group, so it contains no subgroup of order $3$, $5$, $60$ or $120$ (V212 T3). The icosahedral symmetry is therefore not a subgroup of the internal link group; it is realised in the geometry of the shift module, and its double cover sits one level up, in the even Clifford algebra: the quaternion units of $\mathrm{Cl}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^3)$ ($d^2=-1$ to $0$, quaternion products to $0$) embed the binary icosahedral group with closure order $120$ and unitarity defect $1.3\times10^{-15}$.

**Nothing in the internal sector moves.** The content of Sections 4 and 5 is the $16$-sector internal module, and it is blind to which shift module carries it (V217). The pseudoscalar and chiral involution are intact ($w^2=-I$ to $0$, $g_1^2=I$ to $0$, $\{g_1,w\}=0$ to $0$, $[w,U_{32}]=0$ to $3.3\times10^{-16}$, sector projectors of rank $16$ and $16$, orthogonal to $0$); the $32$-internal step is an internal scalar, commuting with $1\otimes w$ to $5.0\times10^{-16}$ on the cubic module and $6.7\times10^{-16}$ on the star; the step is block diagonal in the $16/\overline{16}$ split (off-block $4.2\times10^{-16}$ on the $2048$-dimensional star step), its $16$-block *is* the committed step to $3.6\times10^{-15}$, and matter and antimatter are isospectral to $6.2\times10^{-15}$. Of the four CPT candidates the same one holds on both modules — $g_1$ with cell reflection, stage reversal and transpose — at $5.0\times10^{-15}$ (cubic) and $9.7\times10^{-15}$ (star), where $g_1$ alone fails at $2.0$ and $0.18$. The anomaly coefficients are unchanged, and the trace-form ratio is $1.666666667$. The module changes the kinematics, and it costs no quantum number.

**The new module is gapless on lines, and their transverse touching is linear.** The scan of its first zone out to $|\mathbf{q}| = \pi/1.902113 = 1.6516$ covers $125$ momenta, of which $13$ are exactly gapless below $10^{-9}$; the smallest gap anywhere is $8.9\times10^{-18}$ at the zone centre, where $512$ of $1024$ eigenphases vanish while the first-order splitting of that subspace is $1.7\times10^{-13}$, $2.0\times10^{-13}$, $1.3\times10^{-13}$ — no linear term there, so $\Gamma$ stays quadratic on the star module exactly as it is on the cubic one (V215 T3). The new object is not a point but a locus: the gapless momenta sit on the lines through the icosahedron's two-fold axes, and leaving such a line the gap closes with transverse exponent $0.999$ and $0.997$ at $(-0.826,0,0)$, $0.998$ and $0.993$ at $(-0.413,0,0)$, $0.997$ and $0.996$ at $(0,-0.826,0)$, while *along* the line the exponent is $-1.178$ — the gap does not reopen. The three probe rays that miss the axis lines, through $(-0.826,-0.826,-0.826)$, give $0.007$, $0.007$, $0.012$: flat directions on a different locus. On the committed cubic module the same instrument finds $32$ of $128$ eigenphases zero at M with first-order norms $0.603$, $0.603$, $0$ and squares $2.926$, $2.926$, $0$ — not proportional: linear there, but little-group anisotropic, which is Proposition 6 speaking.

**The exclusivity of Proposition 5 is confined to two-band crossings.** Along the new module's lines the step is gapless *and* its tangent algebra does not commute (V216). Measured over $34$ star momenta and $33$ cubic momenta with $\kappa = \max_{ab}\lVert[V_a,V_b]\rVert/\max_a\lVert V_a\rVert^2$ taken on the whole gap subspace — every band at the gap energy, its multiplicity recorded, so the number does not depend on which pair of bands one chooses to call the crossing — the star module's six gapless axis momenta all carry a non-Abelian algebra, $\kappa$ from $8.5$ to $23.1$ at gaps of at most $3.8\times10^{-17}$ and gap-subspace multiplicity $128$ of $1024$; the cubic module's eight zone corners carry $\kappa$ from $2.8$ to $8.9$ at multiplicity $64$, while its $\Gamma$–M line is Abelian at $\kappa = 1.3\times10^{-11}$, which is where Proposition 5 lives. Off the gapless set the non-abelianity falls as a power of the gap, $\kappa \sim \mathrm{gap}^{\alpha}$ with $\alpha = 12.99$ (cubic, correlation $0.66$ over $16$ momenta) and $8.86$ (star, $0.57$ over $26$) — but that law does not extrapolate onto the gapless set, where $\kappa$ is of order ten with the gap at the floor. What the new module does *not* deliver is a cone: at its most non-Abelian gapless point the normalised squares of the tangent generators are $57.111$, $0$, $16.704$ (spread $57.111$) and the largest anticommutator is $84.2$, so $\{S_a,S_b\} = 2\delta_{ab}$ fails by more than an order of magnitude. A linear, non-Abelian, strongly anisotropic touching is what is measured. Whether that anisotropy is the kind that survives coarse graining, whether the lines carry a charge that forbids a mass term, and what the move does to the loop factor of [Paper III Section 2] are the three questions the rest of this subsection answers, and they are answered by measurement.

**The anisotropy survives, and the module buys no protection against it.** The icosahedral group's advantage is a statement about a *point* node: its first anisotropic invariant is degree $6$ against the cubic group's $4$ (V212). A line carries only a two-fold little group, and there the advantage disappears. Measured about the star's two-fold line, the transverse velocity profile on a circle of radius $3\times10^{-3}$ over $96$ azimuths has leading azimuthal degree $2$ — the same leading degree as the cubic $\Gamma$–$M$ line — with total anisotropy $0.680$ of its own mean against the cubic's $0.500$, spanning $v = 1.5\times10^{-4}$ to $1.204$ where the cubic spans $7.5\times10^{-4}$ to $0.816$; the odd-harmonic power is $1.2\times10^{-12}$, which is to say zero, so the profile carries exactly the period $\pi$ of the line's own little group (V219). The harmonic content differs rather than the leading degree: on this module the degree-$4$ harmonic carries the most power ($0.227$ against $0.061$ at degree $2$), while on the cubic line degree $2$ dominates ($0.174$ against $0.035$). Two controls keep the comparison honest. On a $64$-direction sphere the coarse velocity field is if anything *more* isotropic on this module ($\min v/\max v = 4\times10^{-4}$ against $1.3\times10^{-3}$): the difference lives in the profile around the line, which is what a low-energy fermion actually sees. And the line's own two-fold invariance is exact in the linear-response limit — the defect of the velocity under the rotations about the axis falls linearly with the circle radius, $6.88\times10^{-3}$ at $10^{-2}$ and $2.06\times10^{-3}$ at $3\times10^{-3}$, slope $1.0000$ — so the defect seen at fixed radius is the $O(\epsilon)$ correction, not a breaking of the symmetry. The touching is not a cone either: restricted to the two crossing bands the tangent generators have norms $2.7\times10^{-6}$, $0.177$, $1.318$, and the Clifford relation $\{S_a,S_b\} = 2\delta_{ab}\mathbb{1}$ fails at its maximum, defect $2.000$; the cubic $M$ node gives $0.444$, $0.444$, $8\times10^{-15}$ and the same maximal defect, with the vanishing generator in a different slot. What coarse graining sees on both modules is a degree-$2$ anisotropy, quantitatively the worse here — while the lines themselves are honest: the gap stays at the $10^{-16}$ floor along the line and closes transverse to it with exponent $0.995$ (V219).

**There is no charge on the lines, and a symmetry-respecting mass opens them.** The two-band picture would have given a Berry phase of $\pi$ around the line, and with it a prohibition on a mass term. Measured on the module's own step there is nothing band-resolved to measure: at the line the touching is a cluster of $128$ of $1024$ eigenphases at phase zero (the cubic $\Gamma$–$M$ control: $64$), the largest phase inside the cluster at $2.3\times10^{-14}$ with the first one outside at $3.2\times10^{-1}$, and consecutive eigenvectors of the single closest phase overlap at $10^{-14}$ in the worst case — a per-band phase on such a touching is a choice of basis inside the degeneracy (and the eigensolver's basis inside a $128$-fold cluster is non-orthogonal, condition up to $4.4\times10^{2}$, so the frame has to be re-orthonormalised before transport; on the re-orthonormalised frame the transport is unitary to $4\times10^{-4}$) (V220). The cluster is isolated along every circle by a relative gap of at least $5.2$, rising to $1479$ at the smallest radius, so the cluster's parallel transport is a genuine invariant of the step restricted to the touching — and the invariant says there is no charge. The determinant phase of the $128\times128$ holonomy depends on the circle: $-0.0998$ rad at radius $3\times10^{-3}$, $-1.3300$ at $10^{-2}$, $-2.7335$ at $3\times10^{-2}$ in the plane cut across the line, and $-2.2902$, $-1.3300$, $-0.3790$ at the same three radii in a plane tilted $45^\circ$ about it — a spread of $2.63$ rad, where a topological monodromy is constant. The cubic control gives exactly $0.0000$ at the two smallest radii, which is what its Abelian tangent algebra ($\kappa = 1.3\times10^{-11}$, V216) predicts. What falls with the loop is a curvature flux, not a number. The mass test decides the rest: the commutant of the internal map is computed, by Cesàro averaging over $240$ powers of the step (obstruction $4.2\times10^{-15}$), and the unitary perturbation $\exp(-i\theta C)$ built from the measured operator opens a gap *at the line* that grows linearly with the coupling — $1.277\times10^{-4}$ at $\theta = 10^{-3}$ and $1.277\times10^{-3}$ at $\theta = 10^{-2}$, ratio $10.00$. The symmetry available to forbid it is one two-fold rotation: $2$ of the point group's $60$ elements preserve the line direction, and those leave the spectrum at the line fixed to exactly $0$. The transverse exponent read a second way from the cluster, $\beta = 1.006$ across the line and $1.008$ in the tilted plane ($1.000$ for the cubic line), agrees with V219's $0.995$. These lines are exact and linearly dispersive, and they are stable as loci and unstable as particles.

**The spin can enter the module only through its bond layer, and one covariant assignment has a node.** The committed step factorises, transport against internal layer, and a factorised step cannot carry a non-Abelian tangent algebra on a degenerate subspace. Since Sections 4 and 5 are blind to which module carries the internal sector (V217), the change has to be made in what a hop *carries*: on the star's twelve directed axes let the bond operator be $U_d = -\hat v_d\cdot\vec\sigma$ — Hermitian, an involution, a vector under the binary icosahedral group, with $U_{-d} = -U_d$. The covariance is exact over the $120$ lifts, $D(R)U_dD(R)^\dagger = U_{\sigma_R(d)}$ to $3.5\times10^{-15}$, with $U_d^2 = I$ to $2.2\times10^{-16}$ and $U_{-d}+U_d$ to $0$, and the star geometry is the module V214 measured: six equiangular axes, one cosine $0.4472136 = 1/\sqrt5$, isotropic fourth moment to $2.7\times10^{-15}$ where the cubic axes give $0.400$ (V225 T1). Covariance alone does not choose it. The quaternionic assignment intertwines just as exactly ($3.5\times10^{-15}$) but its bonds square to $-I$, and its step has no node at all: a zone-centre gap of $1.571$. The scalar assignment — factorised kinematics, no spin — is trivially covariant with $U_{-d} = U_d$ (defect $2.0$). What separates them is the node, not the covariance.

**The cell breaks inversion, and that is the price of the linear term.** At the zone centre the step is invariant under $2I$ to $2.5\times10^{-15}$, while the inversion defect is $1.41421 = \sqrt2$: inversion is not a symmetry of this step. That is Proposition 6 read from the other side. On the scalar-bond control inversion is intact (defect $0$) and the edge of the phase spread that opens does so quadratically (exponent $2.000$); on the Clifford cell the spread is linear in the radius, exponent $1.0000$ in every one of the $19$ tracked directions, with band velocities at radius $10^{-2}$ running from $0.0138$ to $1.3441$ (V225 T5). The equivariant census at the node's little group says what the symmetry allows: $\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{2I}(D,\,D\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^nV_3)$ for $n\le 8$ is $[1,1,1,1,1,2,3,3,3]$, the isotropic invariants $[1,0,1,0,1,0,2,0,2]$, the primitive equivariants $[1,1,0,0,0,1,1,0,0]$, and the first anisotropy is pushed to degree $5$ against the cubic module's $4$. The degree-$0$ count is $1$: a mass term is allowed by the point group, so whatever protects this node has to be a charge (V225 T1) — and V226 and V227 measure that it is not there.

**The node is arithmetic, its gap subspace is the coin spinor times the internal sector, and the Clifford identity survives on one piece of it.** With the Clifford layer the kinetic phases at the zone centre are pinned to $\{0,\pi\}$ independently of the clock, so the gap closes exactly when the internal map rides the block layer at exponent $0$ or $4\bmod 8$: $U_{16}^8 - I = 4.9\times10^{-15}$ against $U_{16}^4 - I = 2.0$, and the measured table gives $192$ zero eigenphases of $384$ at exponents $0,4,12$ and gaps of $0.785$, $1.571$, $0.785$ at exponents $1,2,3$ — $14$ gapless entries of the $42$ (assignment, layers, exponent) combinations tried (V225 T2). The $192$-vector gap cluster (largest $\lvert\sin\theta\rvert$ inside it $7.6\times10^{-15}$) splits, by character projectors built from the group rather than from a table, as $\mathrm{sym}^1\oplus\mathrm{sym}^3\oplus\mathrm{sym}^5$ with multiplicity $16$ each, ranks $32$, $64$, $96$, idempotency defects at most $1.3\times10^{-15}$ (V225 T3). On the cluster as a whole the tangent algebra is the Abelian case V216 excluded: the three generators share one norm, $10.759976$ to a spread of $3.6\times10^{-15}$, commute to $2.1\times10^{-14}$, and have a direction-independent velocity spectrum — levels $0$, $0.707107$, $1.144123$, each $64$-fold. The Clifford structure is not there; it lives on the spinor piece. Restricted to $\mathrm{sym}^1$ ($32$-dimensional) the off-diagonal anticommutators fall to $8.0\times10^{-16}$, $V_j^2 = 0.201002\,I$ to $5.8\times10^{-16}$, and the three directions share one speed, $0.448332$: the test $\{S_a,S_b\} = 2\delta_{ab}$, which failed by $2.000$ at the star's zone centre and $0.444$ at the cubic $M$ node (V215), holds — on $32$ of the $192$ gapless directions (V225 T4b).

**The gapless set of the cell is three lines, the module has no Brillouin zone to double over, and nothing on it carries a charge.** A $1791$-point grid over $\lvert q\rvert\le\pi/\ell = 1.6516$ ($\ell = 1.902113$, the generator length V214 measured) has smallest gap $0.0$ on a coordinate axis; $40$ grid points sit below $10^{-8}$ and $43$ below $10^{-2}$, the $12$ components the grid can resolve are all pierced by gapless rays ($10$ to $18$ of its $42$ mesh directions) and none is isolated. The directions the gapless points lie on collect into three lines, probed further out along them: two stay gapless at every probe, the third at all but one ($2.6\times10^{-8}$, V227 T1). The doubling theorem's hypothesis is absent rather than satisfied: written in the basis of three of them, the twelve directions give a coefficient matrix with entries in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$, and the integrality search over $\lvert n\rvert\le 3$ bottoms out at defect $0.1459$, so no nonzero $G$ is a period of the step; what the module does offer is a shortest rational hop, norm $2.0000$, displacement $[0,0,2]$, $17$ steps (V227 T4). And there is no charge to hide behind: no sphere about any of the $12$ locus points keeps its link rank (smallest overlaps $2\times10^{-17}$ to $6\times10^{-16}$, the readings pinned at integer or half-integer defects), and of the nine spheres about the zone centre of V226 exactly one keeps rank while the ladder's readings run over $5,12,15,16,25,34$. The reason is measured, not assumed: on the icosahedral mesh's $92$ directions no sphere about the zone centre is pierced at all — the largest gap on the three sampled spheres is $1.8\times10^{-3}$, $6.0\times10^{-3}$, $2.4\times10^{-2}$ — while probing the cell's own six signed coordinate axes instead, $4$, $5$ and $6$ of the $6$ come back gapless at the same three radii (largest line gap $2.6\times10^{-8}$). So every sphere is cut by a line the mesh simply misses, and a flux read on such a mesh is an artefact of the mesh (V226 T0b, T1). The control pins the failure on the cell rather than on the instrument: the spinless-bond cell's sphere about the same zone centre keeps full rank (overlap $0.9998$) and returns exactly $0$, defect $8\times10^{-16}$ (V227 T5).

**With no charge, an invariant mass is allowed, and it opens the touching linearly.** Projecting a random Hermitian operator onto the invariants of the $2I$ action leaves an obstruction of $1.4\times10^{-15}$ and a generator of norm $7.31$: the invariant class is not empty, exactly as the degree-$0$ equivariant count predicted. Switching it on as an on-site potential, $S(q)\mapsto e^{-i\theta V}S(q)$, moves the zone-centre gap from $8.046\times10^{-6}$ at $\theta = 10^{-3}$ to $8.045\times10^{-5}$ at $\theta = 10^{-2}$ and $8.021\times10^{-4}$ at $\theta = 10^{-1}$: opening linear in the coupling, gap over coupling constant to $0.3\%$ over the scan. The minimum over the scan relocates off the zone centre ($3.6\times10^{-6}$ at radius $5\times10^{-3}$), so the mass opens the lines without removing them. The matched scalar-bond control, whose touching is quadratic (edge exponent exactly $2.0$, V225 T6), is opened by the same class as well — $2.03\times10^{-6}$, $2.14\times10^{-5}$, $3.24\times10^{-4}$ — but at a rate that grows with the coupling rather than the cell's constant one, while on the spheres it can read its charge stays exactly zero (V226 T3, T4). Nothing about the touching is protected: the same verdict V220 reached for the star's own lines, on a cell that carries spin.

**The bond layer leaves the internal sector whole, and it does not leave the CPT operator or the loop return alone.** The step commutes with $\mathbb 1\otimes w$ to $7.1\times10^{-31}$, is block diagonal in the pseudoscalar basis (off-block norms $2.9\times10^{-16}$), its $16$-block agrees spectrally with the step built directly on the $16$ sectors to $4.0\times10^{-15}$ — the reuse of $U_{16}$ is a consequence, not a choice — and its matter and antimatter blocks are isospectral to $3.8\times10^{-15}$: CPT on the spectrum carries over to the cell that carries a spinor bond layer, as it did on the star module (V217). At the operator level it does not carry over. Over the full candidate set the best defect on the Clifford cell is $1.1$ and no candidate holds, and the identity that holds on the scalar-bond control to $5.0\times10^{-16}$, $\mathrm P g_1 S(q) g_1^{-1}(\mathrm P g_1)^{-1} = S(-q)$, fails there by exactly $1.41421$ — the same number as the inversion defect V225 measured at the zone centre (V228 T3). The internal data the paper reads off that sector are untouched: the $5/3$ trace-form ratio stays $1.6666666667$, the eight anomaly coefficients stay zero (largest $2.2\times10^{-16}$), $32 = 2\times16$ (V228 T4). The loop return changes structurally. $F_n = \langle\mathrm{tr}\,S(q)^n\rangle/d$ over the momentum ball on grids $5,9,17$ ($2109$ points) splits by parity: every odd $n\le 12$ vanishes in modulus to between $3\times10^{-18}$ and $9\times10^{-18}$, while the even ones alternate $0.460$, $-0.334$, $-0.294$, $0.212$, $0.209$, $-0.181$; the matched scalar-bond control is nonzero at every length, odd ones included. The spinor bond layer thus kills the odd returns of the cell — the same odd-even structure the closed branch shows for the loop ratios at the root angle (V203 T1) — and at the grids run its even returns are not converged (refinement changes $3\times10^{-2}$ to $2.3\times10^{-1}$, of the same size as the values), so the loop factor of [Paper III Section 2] has to be rebuilt on this cell before it can be read from it (V228 T5).

## 9. The quantized clock: the root closes its own parameter

The parameter audit of [Paper III Section 5] classes the rotation angle among the construction's parameters — a free continuous number of the dynamics, fixed by the convention $g = 1/n_{\rm qubits}$. There is a sharper condition, and the algebra itself enforces it. A cellular-automaton ontology demands that the fundamental update be *algebraic*, not analytic: the step must be an element of the root — a member of the Clifford group generated by the $h_a$, which for the plane-rotation product means $e^{ig}$ a root of unity. Call this the **closure axiom**: no continuous phase may enter the dynamics.

**Theorem 6 (the two closed steps).** *Over every rational angle $g = p\pi/q$ ($q \le 16$) and every candidate of the proposed rule ($1/5$, $\pi/5$, $\pi/10$, $\pi/8$, $3\pi/8$, $\pi/6$, $\pi/3$), the internal step $U(g)$ is exactly periodic at precisely two angles: $g = \pi/2$, of order $4$, and $g = \pi/4$, of order $8$. At $g = 1/5$ the internal clock is irrational: the smallest level is $0.011466$ rad per step, $2\pi/\omega_{\min} = 548.0$, and no order $\le 64$ exists (deviation $0.45$).*

*Proof of the $\pi/2$ case, and a stronger statement.* Each plane rotation at the half angle is $-iT_{ab}$, and $T_{ab} = i h_a h_b$, so $U(\pi/2) = (-i)^{45} i^{45} \prod_{a<b} h_a h_b = \prod_{a<b} h_a h_b$. The product of all $45$ pairs in ascending order reduces — numerically to $6.1\times10^{-17}$ in the $1024$-element algebra basis, with every other basis coefficient below $10^{-8}$ — to the single monomial $h_0 h_1 \cdots h_9$, the pseudoscalar $w$; since $w^2 = -I$, $U(\pi/2)^4 = I$. The identity is *ordering-independent*: the monomial content of any ordering of the $45$ factors is $\pm w$, because each $h_k$ appears in exactly nine pairs, and five shuffled orderings reproduce the order-$4$ to $9.8\times10^{-16}$. The step at the half angle *is* the root's chirality operator.

**The quantized step ($g = \pi/4$).** $U(\pi/4)^4 = -I$ and $U(\pi/4)^8 = I$ exactly ($5.0\times10^{-15}$), and the $32$ internal levels are exactly the algebraic set

$$\omega \in \{\pm\tfrac{\pi}{4}\ (10),\ \pm\tfrac{3\pi}{4}\ (6)\},\qquad \max_k\big|\omega_k - k\pi/4\big| < 3\times10^{-15},$$

$\pm$-paired, zero point below $2\times10^{-15}$, $\det U = 1$: the internal spectrum becomes pure algebra, with no continuous number anywhere. The invariants that hold for every $g$ (the $16/\overline{16}$ split, the mirror, $\det U = 1$) are untouched. The order-$8$ requires the canonical Appendix-A ordering of the $45$ factors — five shuffled orderings break it (deviation $\sim 1$) — so the ordering, already an input of the step, carries the quantization.

**The full step closes too.** At $g = \pi/4$ the exact one-step Bloch matrix of the $16$ sector at the zone centre satisfies $M(0)^2 = I$ below $1\times10^{-14}$ (and on the full $256$-dimensional module below $1\times10^{-14}$; $16$-sector closure leak below $5\times10^{-16}$): the fundamental dynamics is a finite-state machine, 't Hooft's determinism exact at the fundamental update. The doubler defect of Section 8.3 is repaired, but the repaired census is not a set of points. At $g = \pi/4$ the exactly gapless set of the block zone is $\Gamma$ together with the **six face-diagonal lines** $\{(\pm t, \pm t, 0)\}$ and its axis permutations: every point of every line is gapless below $6.7\times10^{-16}$ over its whole length, the twelve M points are only the high-symmetry points that happen to sit on them (their gaps $0$–$4.4\times10^{-16}$), X and R stay gapped at $0.5236$, the body diagonal is gapped away from $\Gamma$ ($0.022$ at $t = 0.3$), and none of the gapless points of a $15^3$ grid lies off the lines — where the implemented angle finds no gapless point at all. The step still factorises as $M(q) = P(q) \otimes U_{16}^{6}$ there ($3.6\times10^{-15}$).

The touching is **quadratic**: through an M point the gap stays zero along the line, grows linearly in one transverse direction (slope $0.577$) and quadratically in the other, and the three axis exponents at every one of the twelve nodes are a permutation of $(1,1,2)$. The chirality bookkeeping of Proposition 2 then fails as stated: it needs isolated zeros, and at $12/12$ M nodes the gap along the line through the node stays below $10^{-9}$ at steps $10^{-3}, 10^{-2}, 10^{-1}$ — the charges whose sum is exactly $0$ at $g = 1/5$ become sign noise, and their naive sum is not quantized ($-2$ or $-6$ depending on the arithmetic route), so the Nielsen–Ninomiya count must be redone per line, not per node.

## Appendix A. The explicit 32-dimensional representation

The ten generators are built on five qubits by the standard symplectic Pauli pattern: for $k = 0,\dots,4$,

$$g_{2k} = Y^{\otimes k}\otimes X\otimes I^{\otimes(4-k)},\qquad g_{2k+1} = Y^{\otimes k}\otimes Z\otimes I^{\otimes(4-k)}.$$

On the same qubit slot $X$ and $Z$ anticommute; across slots, the $Y^{\otimes k}$ prefix makes a generator at slot $k$ anticommute with any generator at slot $j<k$. This gives exactly ten mutually anticommuting Hermitian matrices, $g_a^2 = I$, on a $32$-dimensional complex space. The algebra basis is the $1024$ ordered products $g_{a_1}\cdots g_{a_r}$, $0 \le r \le 10$; the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ grading is the parity of $r$. The canonical ordering of the $45$ plane-rotation factors $T_{ab}$ used throughout is the ascending lexicographic order on $(a,b)$; the order-$8$ closure of the quantized step is specific to it.

The two singlet sectors of the cell module are the four further one-dimensional slots on which every generator acts as $0$; the internal layer multiplies them by $e^{\pm i\,\mathrm{loop}^3}$ and $e^{\pm i\,\mathrm{loop}^{10}}$ per stage — conjugate pairs at opposite phases (Axiom 1). Every numerical statement in this paper is computed on exactly this representation, and the companion release of Appendix B is its sole producer.
