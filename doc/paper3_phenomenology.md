### 3.3 Baryon asymmetry

The automaton alone cannot produce it. Every CP-odd (T-odd) invariant of the unitary step vanishes: $\mathrm{Im}\,\mathrm{Tr}$ of all two- and three-fold products of the $\mathbf{45}$, of a $2\times10^5$ sample of four-fold products, of all $10\times10\times45\times45$ baryon-violating sandwiches, and of the rank-1 real democratic Yukawa — all zero to $10^{-15}$. The step is unitary (in equilibrium), baryon-conserving (the $\mathbf{45}$ commute with $w$) and CP-conserving. It satisfies **none** of the Sakharov conditions [12]. This is a computed theorem, and it is the correct starting point. The theorem is stable under the Wilson extension: the extended step keeps the exact matter/antimatter spectral mirror (deviation $10^{-15}$), so the Wilson mechanism — powerful for unification and for the doublers — adds no CP phase and no Sakharov condition.

A GUT-leptogenesis estimate [13] is then built by hand:

$$\Delta B = \frac{28}{79}\,\varepsilon_{CP}\,D,\qquad \varepsilon_{CP} = \frac{g_0^4}{16\pi} = 4.9\times10^{-6},\qquad D = \Big(\frac{1}{16\pi}\Big)^2 = 3.96\times10^{-4},$$

with $g_0 = 1/2^3 = 1/8$ and the Standard Model sphaleron conversion $B = \tfrac{28}{79}(B-L)$ [14,15], giving $\Delta B = 6.8\times10^{-10}$ against the measured $(6.12\pm0.04)\times10^{-10}$ [16] — a ratio $1.11$.

Three problems. (i) $\varepsilon_{CP}$ is not a computed vertex: the physical CP asymmetry of $N\to \ell H$ is a one-loop self-energy/vertex function of the heavy-neutrino masses, and $g_0^4/16\pi$ is a dimensional guess. (ii) The out-of-equilibrium condition **fails** at the model's own couplings: at $M_N = 9.6\times10^{13}$ GeV, $H = 1.66\sqrt{g_*}M_N^2/M_P = 1.298\times10^{10}$ GeV while $\Gamma_N = y^2 M_N/8\pi = 5.98\times10^{10}$ GeV for $y = 1/8$ ($1.93\times10^{12}$ GeV for $y = 0.71$), so $\Gamma_N/H \simeq 4.6$ ($\simeq 148$). Out-of-equilibrium decay requires $y \lesssim 0.06$, which the theory does not select. (iii) $28/79$ is an input of the Standard Model sector, not a number of the root. The $11\%$ agreement is therefore a coincidence of chosen factors (O5). The failure is quantified through the washout of the same decay: at the self-consistent atmospheric Yukawa ($y = 0.40$, the one that gives $m_\nu = 0.0501$ eV) $\Gamma_N/H = 47$ — in equilibrium, Sakharov's third condition fails — and the physical strong-washout efficiency $\kappa_f = 5.5\times10^{-3}$ [13] sits $14\times$ away from the guessed dilution $(1/16\pi)^2$: the agreement is an accident of two wrong factors, not a near-miss.

A sharper statement is available. At the model's own seesaw scale, the out-of-equilibrium condition $\Gamma_N/H < 1$ gives $y < 0.058$. The same Yukawa fixes the light neutrino mass through the same seesaw,

$$m_\nu = \frac{y^2 v^2}{M_N} \le 2.1\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{eV}\qquad (y \le 0.058),$$

a bound of the same kind and size as the standard thermal-leptogenesis bound [17]. The atmospheric splitting requires $m_\nu \simeq 0.050$ eV, i.e. $y \simeq 0.28$ with $m_D = yv$ and $v = 246$ GeV ($y \simeq 0.40$ in the $v_u = 174$ GeV convention), for which $\Gamma_N/H \simeq 23$. **At $M_N = 9.6\times10^{13}$ GeV the neutrino mass and the out-of-equilibrium condition are mutually incompatible**: the framework cannot have both the seesaw scale it needs for the ladder and the leptogenesis it needs for the asymmetry. ### 3.5 Flavour

## 4. Cosmology

### 4.1 Dark energy

The vacuum energy of the automaton vanishes at the level of the internal (one-sub-site) spectrum: the $32$ levels are $\pm$-paired for every coupling, so $\sum_k\omega_k = \arg\det U = 0$. This is the correct, non-vacuous form of the cancellation. (Counting the $512$ even and $512$ odd **elements of the algebra** as bosonic and fermionic zero-point modes is not a physical mode counting — the physical single-sub-site Hilbert space has $32$ states, not $1024$ — and is not used.)

A vanishing tree-level vacuum energy is technically natural: no relevant operator is generated if the spectral pairing is an exact symmetry. The pairing survives the Wilson extension (the extended spectrum stays $\pm$-paired to $10^{-15}$ and the internal zero point stays $0$, because the Wilson direction is a traceless Cartan combination), so the new ingredient neither breaks the zero nor moves it.

The zero is **protected, not accidental**. Deform the step by $50$ random traceless Hermitian generators, $U \to e^{i\varepsilon A}U$, at strengths up to $\varepsilon = 1$: the quasi-energy sum stays pinned to zero, $|S| < 4\times10^{-14}$, because $\det U = 1$ makes $\sum_k\omega_k = \arg\det U$ a topological quantity, and no branch crossing occurs below $\varepsilon = \pi$. The only internal knob that moves the sum is the $\mathrm{U}(1)$ trace phase.

What the theory does **not** do is produce the observed value. The observed $\Lambda \sim 10^{-122}M_P^4$ lies $122$ orders of magnitude *below* the classical bound $M_P^4$ and *above* the theory's spectral zero, and no mechanism breaking the pairing by that amount is known. Independently, the classical self-gravitating de Sitter vacuum of [Paper II Section 3] has $G_{\mu\nu} = -3H^2 g_{\mu\nu}$, i.e. a vacuum energy bounded by $M_P^4$ on the lattice. These are three different statements — spectral zero, classical bound, observed value — and they do not "reconcile": the gap between the first and the third is O1, the largest unexplained number in the theory [27]. The equation of state of a cosmological-constant vacuum is $w = -1$ by definition, not by derivation.

The problem is a single named number: the observed $\Lambda$ is **equivalent to** a trace-phase breaking of size

$$\varepsilon\operatorname{Tr}A \sim 16\pi^2\times10^{-122} = 1.6\times10^{-120}.$$

O1 is not "why is the vacuum energy small" but "what breaks the trace $\mathrm{U}(1)$ by $10^{-120}$".

The observed baryon asymmetry is not explained: Section 3.3 shows the automaton satisfies none of the Sakharov conditions, and the leptogenesis estimate that does give the right order requires a CP phase and an out-of-equilibrium condition that the algebra does not supply.
