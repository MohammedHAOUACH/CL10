## Abstract

A unitary quantum cellular automaton on a Planck lattice is constructed whose cell is a $2{\times}2{\times}2$ block of sites, each of its eight sub-sites carrying the $32$-dimensional complex module of the Clifford algebra $\mathrm{Cl}(10,0)$ — on-site space $\mathbb{C}^{256}$. Ten mutually anticommuting Hermitian generators $h_a^2 = I$ are the whole of the kinematics. From this single object follow a $(1{+}3)$ Minkowski subalgebra; a $16$-component Weyl module whose charge assignment is exactly one Standard Model generation, with all eight gauge and mixed anomalies cancelling; the $45$-dimensional $\mathrm{Spin}(10)$ adjoint containing the twelve gauge bosons; the $5/3$ hypercharge normalisation; an exactly unitary, strictly causal update with $\det U = 1$, a $\pm$-paired spectrum and an exact matter/antimatter spectral mirror; and a lattice gravitational sector that reproduces general relativity on de Sitter, Schwarzschild and $3{+}1$ data to machine precision. Requiring the update to be an element of the root — the closure axiom — quantizes the one continuous parameter of the dynamics to two angles, $\pi/2$ and $\pi/4$, and the finer of the two is the physical branch: the fundamental step has finite order and the internal spectrum is a finite algebraic set.

The paper states equally precisely what does **not** follow. Every number is classified by provenance — *algebraic*, *conventional*, *fitted*, or *open*. The family number, the Yukawa structure, quark mixing, the cosmological constant, the dark-matter identity and relic abundance, the neutrino mass, the baryon asymmetry and the values of the gauge couplings remain open, each with the size of the gap quantified. One loop factor $(1/16\pi)^n$ organises four scales over thirty orders of magnitude: $n = 10$ reproduces the electroweak/Planck hierarchy to a factor $2.08$ and fixes a $118$ GeV dark-matter scale, $n = 3$ the seesaw, $n = 17$ the neutrino mass; the loop factor itself is reproduced to $3.6\%$ by a closed traced loop of length $10$ in the automaton's own gauged bond layer, and the crossing of $1/(16\pi)$ occurs at exactly $n = 10$.

Seven tensions with observation are reported rather than hidden: under Standard-Model desert running the coupling $\alpha_{10} = 1/25$ overshoots the measured $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ coupling by a factor $5.8$ and drives $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ to a Landau pole at $2.2\times10^{9}$ GeV; the bare neutrino ladder is excluded by $\sum m_\nu < 0.12$ eV; no state of the implemented spectrum can be the dark matter; the relic abundance computed from the couplings the root supplies is not $0.120$; the string tension of the gauged layer is $5.4\times10^{40}$ times the QCD value; the implemented step has no exactly massless one-particle state and therefore no graviton candidate; and the node cones of the lattice spectrum are anisotropic nodal planes, not light cones. Nine open problems and seven falsifiers are stated with their numbers.

**Keywords.** Clifford algebra; quantum cellular automaton; $\mathrm{Spin}(10)$ grand unification; anomaly cancellation; lattice gravity; dark matter; cosmological constant; baryogenesis; falsifiability.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The picture

Two habits of language do most of the misleading. The first calls $c$ the speed of light. Light is one traveller at that speed; the constant is the speed of causality. The second is wave–particle duality, taught as a paradox. On a lattice it dissolves: there is one wave, always; what a detector registers is a single localised event, because the detector is itself a lattice of cells changing state one cell at a time. The ontology contains no particles — only waves that deliver their energy in quanta.

Quantum field theory replaced particles by fields but multiplied the fields, one per species, glued together by hand. The viewpoint here is the opposite one, elementary in computer science: a cellular automaton — a lattice of cells, each holding a finite packet of information, all updated in parallel by one local rule. If the world is such an automaton, everything physical is a wave of information over that lattice, and the theory reduces to two questions: what is the information structure of a single cell, and what is the rule that updates it. Causal Clifford Theory (CL10) answers both concretely: the cell is the spinor module of $\mathrm{Cl}(10,0)$, and the rule is a product of $\mathrm{Spin}(10)$ plane rotations interleaved with nearest-neighbour swap-rotations.

### 1.2 Two literatures, one construction

Discrete models of quantum dynamics and grand unification have developed independently. Causal sets, lattice gauge theory and quantum cellular automata treat spacetime as a finite, causal, unitary dynamical system [1–5]. The observation that one generation of quarks and leptons fits exactly into the $16$-dimensional spinor of $\mathrm{Spin}(10)$, with the $5/3$ hypercharge normalisation and anomaly cancellation following from the embedding, has been standard since 1974 [6–9]; Clifford-algebra formulations of this unification exist [10–12]. 't Hooft's cellular-automaton interpretation supplies a deterministic unitary dynamics but no unification [29,30].

CL10 puts these on a single footing: the internal space of every sub-site of the automaton's cell **is** the spinor module of $\mathrm{Cl}(10,0)$, and the update **is** an element of the corresponding spin group. Nothing else is added by hand: no extra scalar, no extra gauge factor, no second algebra.

### 1.3 Claims, non-claims, and the provenance discipline

**Claimed and demonstrated.** The kinematics of one Standard Model generation, its anomaly freedom, the grand-unified gauge content and the $5/3$, and a unitary causal automaton whose gravitational sector reproduces general relativity on the lattice to machine precision (Sections 3–7, 11).

**Claimed as an order-of-magnitude statement.** The electroweak/Planck hierarchy, the dark-matter mass scale, the neutrino mass and the baryon asymmetry, all expressed through the loop factor $(1/16\pi)^n$ with $n$ a small integer (Section 12). The integers are chosen to land near the measured values; the two-digit coefficients are read from the data.

**Not claimed.** A derivation of the family number, of the fermion masses and mixings, of the cosmological constant, of the dark-matter particle, of the baryon asymmetry, of the spin-2 character of the graviton, of diffeomorphism invariance, or of the Born rule. Each is listed in Section 17 with the size of the gap quantified.

The organising discipline of the paper is therefore a **provenance discipline**: a number is *algebraic* if the root fixes it, *conventional* if it comes from a mapping or scale identification, *fitted* if a coefficient was read from data, and *open* if the theory does not fix it. Section 16 tabulates every headline number under this classification.

## 2. The axiom

**Axiom 1 (the root).** *The universe is a $(1{+}3)$-dimensional causal quantum cellular automaton. Its cell is a $2{\times}2{\times}2$ block of Planck sites — site spacing $\ell_P$, so the cells tile a cubic lattice of spacing $2\ell_P$ — and each of the eight sub-sites carries a copy of the Clifford algebra $\mathrm{Cl}(10,0)$: ten mutually anticommuting Hermitian generators*

$$h_a^\dagger = h_a,\qquad h_a^2 = I,\qquad \{h_a,h_b\} = 0 \quad (a\ne b),\qquad a,b = 1,\dots,10.$$

*The state of the universe is a tensor product of these modules over the sub-sites, so the cell's on-site space is $\mathbb{C}^{256}$, and the dynamics is one time-independent unitary **step** of six **stages** built from the algebra (Section 7.1). The elementary time is the stage, $t_P$; one step is $6t_P$.*

**Why the cell is a block.** The bond part of the update pairs neighbouring sub-sites, and the pairing that treats the three directions alike has six stages: three pair sub-sites *inside* the cell, three pair sub-sites *across* cells. Written on a single Planck site the same rule alternates between bonds inside and outside the site, which forces a two-cell spatial period and a six-periodic time dependence; on the block it is one time-independent rule, and the block is the smallest region on which the update acts as a single unitary. The staggering is therefore not a time dependence of the law but the sub-lattice structure of the cell, and the Bloch decomposition of the step is exact at every step.

Three structural facts about the root, all standard [10,18]:

1. $\mathrm{Cl}(10,0)$ is $2^{10} = 1024$-dimensional; its complexification is $M_{32}(\mathbb{C})$, so it has a single irreducible complex module of dimension $32$, the Dirac module.
2. The volume element $w = h_1 h_2 \cdots h_{10}$ satisfies
   $$w^2 = (-1)^{10\cdot 9/2}\prod_a h_a^2 = (-1)^{45} = -I,\qquad w^\dagger = -w,$$
   so $w$ is anti-Hermitian with eigenvalues $\pm i$, each of multiplicity $16$. The projectors $P_{16} = \tfrac12(I - i w)$ and $P_{\overline{16}} = \tfrac12(I + i w)$ split the Dirac module into two inequivalent $16$-dimensional Weyl modules.
3. The algebra is $\mathbb{Z}_2$-graded by the parity of the number of generators in a product: $512$ even and $512$ odd elements.

Everything derived below is representation theory of this one object plus the dynamics of Section 7.

**Why $10$.** The number $10$ is the smallest $n$ for which a single complex Clifford algebra simultaneously provides (i) a pseudoscalar splitting a Dirac module into two *inequivalent* Weyl modules of dimension $16$ — at $n = 8$ the Weyl modules are $8$-dimensional ($\mathrm{Spin}(8)$ triality: $8_v, 8_s, 8_c$; the $16$-dimensional representations are reducible) — and (ii) an adjoint of dimension $\binom{10}{2} = 45$ large enough to contain $\mathfrak{su}(5)$ and hence the Standard Model algebra. At $n = 9$, $\mathrm{Cl}_9(\mathbb{C}) \simeq M_{16}\oplus M_{16}$ does have $16$-dimensional irreducibles, but they are the two simple factors of the algebra rather than a pseudoscalar split, and $\mathfrak{so}(9)$ does not contain $\mathfrak{su}(5)$. The root is therefore selected by the requirement of a single chiral generation with a unified gauge algebra, not chosen.

## 3. Spacetime as a subalgebra

**Theorem 1.** *Let $T = h_1$ and $S_a = i h_{a+1}$ for $a = 1,2,3$. Then*
$$T^2 = +I,\qquad S_a^2 = -I,\qquad \{T,S_a\} = 0,\qquad \{S_a,S_b\} = 0 .$$
*The real span of $\{T, S_1, S_2, S_3\}$ inside the grade-1 part of the algebra, with the quadratic form induced by the Clifford product, is Minkowski space $\mathbb{R}^{1,3}$.*

*Proof.* The four generators $h_1,\dots,h_4$ anticommute by Axiom 1. Multiplication by $i$ turns $h_a^2 = I$ into $(i h_a)^2 = -I$ and preserves anticommutation. For $v = v_0 T + \sum_a v_a S_a$ with real coefficients, $v^2 = (v_0^2 - \sum_a v_a^2)I$. $\blacksquare$

**Corollary 1 (the light cone).** *A vector $v$ in that real span is null, $v^2 = 0$, if and only if $v_0^2 = \sum_a v_a^2$. The null cone of this quadratic form is the light cone of the lattice.*

Two qualifications. The null cone is a **cone in the grade-1 part**, not a subalgebra: it is not closed under the Clifford product, since $(T+S_1)(T-S_1) = 2I - 2TS_1$ is a grade-0 plus grade-2 element. And the choice of *which* four of the ten generators play the role of spacetime is a choice of frame in the $10$-dimensional generator space, not a prediction: the algebra supplies a family of equivalent $(1{+}3)$ subalgebras related by orthogonal transformations of the generator space. The remaining six generators are inert under the Lorentz generators $\Sigma_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac14[\Gamma_\mu,\Gamma_\nu]$ built from $\Gamma_\mu \in \{T,S_a\}$.

**Causality is a postulate with a dynamical content.** The light cone by itself does not bound signal propagation: a unitary on-site rotation alone has infinite propagation speed. The bound is supplied by the update of Section 7, where the causal radius is *implemented* by the bond stages and *measured* to be exactly one Planck cell per stage (Theorem 5). The light cone and the causal radius are distinct statements, and only the second is a property of the dynamics.

## 4. Matter: the 16 Weyl spinor

**Proposition 1.** *Under $\mathrm{SU}(3)_c\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$ the $16$-dimensional Weyl module is the six left-handed fields of one Standard Model generation:*

$$16 = Q(\mathbf{3},\mathbf{2},+\tfrac16) \oplus u^c(\bar{\mathbf{3}},\mathbf{1},-\tfrac23) \oplus d^c(\bar{\mathbf{3}},\mathbf{1},+\tfrac13) \oplus L(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2},-\tfrac12) \oplus e^c(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1},+1) \oplus \nu^c(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1},0),$$

*with $6+3+3+2+1+1 = 16$. Under $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ this is $\mathbf{10}\oplus\bar{\mathbf{5}}\oplus\mathbf{1}$, the $\mathbf{1}$ being the sterile $\nu^c$.*

The assignment is the standard one [6–9,12]. What the automaton adds is that the $\mathbf{16}$ is not put in by hand but is the Weyl eigenspace of the pseudoscalar of the same algebra that carries the gauge group and the spacetime.

**$B-L$.** $B-L$ is a generator of $\mathfrak{so}(10)$, so it is *not* a single number on the $\mathbf{16}$. Its values on the six fields are $Q: \tfrac13$, $u^c: -\tfrac13$, $d^c: -\tfrac13$, $L: -1$, $e^c: +1$, $\nu^c: +1$; the $\overline{\mathbf{16}}$ carries the negatives of all of them. Under $\mathrm{Spin}(10) \supset \mathrm{SU}(5)\times \mathrm{U}(1)_\chi$, $16 \to \mathbf{10}_{-1}\oplus\bar{\mathbf{5}}_{+2}\oplus\mathbf{1}_{+3}$, and $B-L$ is a linear combination of $\chi$ and the diagonal $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ generator, not $\chi$ itself.

**Theorem 2 (anomaly cancellation).** *All eight gauge and mixed anomaly coefficients of the $\mathbf{16}$ vanish.*

|---|---|---|

| $[\mathrm{SU}(2)]^3$ | $A(\mathbf 2) = 0$ identically ($d^{abc}=0$; the fundamental is pseudo-real) | $0$ |

This is the hardest consistency condition a chiral gauge theory has, and it is satisfied identically by the module content. It is the strongest single result of the theory, and it is independent of the coupling: the cancellation is a property of the algebra, not of a tuned parameter.

**The mass term.** A grade-1 element of the algebra anticommutes with $w$, so it is off-diagonal in the $16/\overline{16}$ split: it maps $16 \to \overline{16}$ and $\overline{16}\to 16$, and both diagonal blocks vanish. A scalar in the $\mathbf{10}$ of $\mathrm{Spin}(10)$ therefore produces a Dirac mass term $16\,\mathbf{10}_H\,\overline{16}$ and no Majorana mass for the charged fermions. The singlet $\nu^c$ is the built-in source of a seesaw (Section 12.3).

Two caveats. A vev in the $\mathbf{10}$ has stabiliser $\mathrm{SO}(9)$: it breaks $\mathrm{Spin}(10)\to\mathrm{SO}(9)$, and $\mathrm{SO}(9)$ is not the Standard Model group. The chain is completed instead by the Wilson direction of Section 8, which at its physical root leaves exactly $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1)^3$ and breaks precisely the leptoquark roots; the two extra $\mathrm{U}(1)$'s must still be broken or hidden. And the $\mathbf{10}$ contains no Standard Model singlet, so the vev that generates masses necessarily breaks the unified group further. This is open problem O6.

**The three families.** The $\mathbf{16}$ is *one* generation. The theory identifies the observed family number with the number $3$ of spatial directions of the $(1{+}3)$ subalgebra, equivalently $\binom{3}{2}$ spatial two-planes, equivalently the rank of the spatial metric: one number stated three ways, not three derivations. The identification has a structural hook: the twelve doubler nodes of Section 8 organise into **three families of four labeled by the zero axis**, one family per spatial direction, with identical one-step spectra (deviation $0$), and the three copies of vectorlike matter required by unification are realizable by taking one node from each family at a single Wilson strength. The index $3$ exists and *labels* the three copies; it does not force the count (O3).

## 5. Gauge: the 45 and the 5/3

The $45$ generators $h_a h_b$ ($a<b$) close on the $\mathrm{Spin}(10)$ adjoint. They are even in the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ grading, hence commute with $w$ and preserve the $16/\overline{16}$ split. Under $\mathrm{Spin}(10)\supset\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$ the $\mathbf{45}$ decomposes as $12$ Standard Model gauge bosons $+$ $12$ leptoquark $X/Y$ bosons $+$ $20$ bosons in the $\mathbf{10}\oplus\overline{\mathbf{10}}$ of $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ $+$ the $\mathrm{U}(1)_{B-L}$ generator; the $33$ broken generators mediate proton decay (Section 13.4).

**Theorem 3 (the $5/3$).** *In the $\mathbf 5$ of $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ the hypercharge is $Y = \mathrm{diag}(-\tfrac13,-\tfrac13,-\tfrac13,+\tfrac12,+\tfrac12)$ with $\mathrm{Tr}\,Y^2 = \tfrac56$, while the non-Abelian generators obey $\mathrm{Tr}\,T_aT_b = \tfrac12\delta_{ab}$. Requiring a single unified coupling $g_5$ for all generators gives $g_1^2/g'^2 = 5/3$, where $g'$ is the Standard Model hypercharge coupling and $g_1 \equiv g_5$.*

*Proof.* $\mathrm{Tr}\,Y^2 = 3\cdot\tfrac19 + 2\cdot\tfrac14 = \tfrac56$. Rescaling $Y \to \sqrt{3/5}\,Y$ normalises it to $\tfrac12$; then $g_5 (\sqrt{3/5}Y) = g' Y$ gives $g' = g_5\sqrt{3/5}$. $\blacksquare$

This is an algebraic fact of the root: no parameter enters. It anchors the coupling discussion of Section 13.1, where it will be seen that the *normalisation* of the unified coupling is under control while its *value* is not.

## 6. Statistics and measurement

The $\mathbb{Z}_2$ grading splits the algebra into $512$ even and $512$ odd elements. The even sector is identified with bosons and the odd sector with fermions. This is a *kinematic* grading of the operator algebra: a postulate of the interpretation, not a derivation of the spin–statistics theorem, and not a claim of dynamical supersymmetry. No supercharge exists in the construction and no superpartner is predicted.

Between measurements the automaton is exactly unitary. A detection is modelled as the standard projection: $\sum_x \psi(x)|x\rangle$ reduces to $|x_0\rangle$ with probability $|\psi(x_0)|^2$, the probability rule being the intensity $|\psi|^2$ of the propagating lattice field — the one-electron-at-a-time build-up of the interference fringes is the Tonomura experiment [47]. The projection is **not** derived from the unitary step and the theory does not claim it is: the reduction postulate is an input of the interpretation (O9). What the automaton adds is only that the wave being sampled is a strictly causal, unitary, lattice field.

## 7. The step: unitarity, spectrum, causality

### 7.1 The update

The step has six stages, and every stage is the same two layers: the internal rotation applied to every sub-site, then one bond stage. The internal factor is built from the $\mathrm{Spin}(10)$ plane-rotation generators $T_{ab} = i\,h_a h_b$ ($a<b$) in the $32$-dimensional module — each $T_{ab}$ Hermitian, squaring to $I$, traceless (sixteen $+1$ and sixteen $-1$ eigenvalues):

$$U(g) \;=\; \prod_{a<b}\Big(\cos g\, I - i \sin g\, T_{ab}\Big).$$

A sub-site is labelled by $e = (e_1,e_2,e_3) \in \{0,1\}^3$, the bit of axis $j$ being $2^j$. A **bond stage** acts on the four disjoint pairs $(e,\, e \oplus 2^j)$: an *intra-block* stage takes the pairs with $e_j = 0$, inside the cell; an *inter-block* stage takes the pairs joining $e_j = 1$ of one cell to $e_j = 0$ of the next cell along $\hat e_j$ — still a two-cell operator, now between neighbouring cells. On each pair the gate is the swap-rotation $\cos g\, I - i\sin g\,(|e\rangle\langle o| + |o\rangle\langle e|)$, carrying the same single angle and no other parameter. The six stages run in the fixed order (intra $x$, inter $y$, intra $z$, inter $x$, intra $y$, inter $z$). One step is therefore

$$M \;=\; \prod_{s=1}^{6} \big(P_s \otimes U(g)\big),$$

an operator on the cell's on-site space $\mathbb{C}^{256}$ ($P_s$ on the eight sub-sites, $U$ on the internal module). At block crystal momentum $q$ an inter-block pair carries the phase $e^{\pm iq}$, $M(q)$ is $256 \times 256$, and on the $16$ sector it is $128 \times 128$.

Every factor is special unitary: the $45$ internal factors have $\det = \exp(-i g\,\mathrm{Tr}\,T_{ab}) = 1$, and each bond pair block $\left(\begin{smallmatrix} c & -is\\ -is & c\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ has determinant $c^2 + s^2 = 1$ (the inter-block phases cancel). Hence

$$U U^\dagger = I,\quad \det U = 1, \qquad M M^\dagger = I,\quad \det M = 1 \qquad \text{for every } g,$$

verified numerically: internal unitarity and $|\det U - 1|$ at the $10^{-15}$ level; for the full cell matrix $\|MM^\dagger - I\| \le 5\times10^{-15}$ and $|\det M - 1| \le 3\times10^{-13}$ at the zone centre, at a generic momentum and at the two high-symmetry points; the real-space six-stage step preserves the norm of a random cell state to $10^{-15}$. Two structural inputs must be named: the $45$ factors do not commute, so $U$ depends on the *ordering* of the product, and the six stages have a fixed order. The algebra does not select either order.

The angle $g$ is the one continuous number of the dynamics. Two readings of it coexist and are kept apart throughout. The **implemented** value $g = 1/5$ is the one on which the phenomenology of Sections 12–14 is computed; it comes from the proposed rule $g = 1/n_{\rm qubits}$, the five qubits of the $32$-dimensional module, which the data do not support (Section 16). The **closed** values $g = \pi/2$ and $g = \pi/4$ are the only ones for which the step is an element of the root (Section 9). The two readings disagree by a measured amount, and both are reported.

### 7.2 The single-particle spectrum

The $32$ eigenphases $\omega_k$ of $U$ (the spectrum of $H = -i\log U$) are the energies of one sub-site per stage, in units of $1/t_P$; the spectrum of the cell at block momentum $q$ is the set of eigenphases of $M(q)$, $128$ of them on the $16$ sector. At $g = 1/5$ the internal levels are

$$\omega \in \{\pm 0.01147,\ \pm 0.05447,\ \pm 0.15819,\ \dots,\ \pm 1.48638\},$$

exactly $\pm$-paired to $2\times10^{-15}$, so the single-sub-site vacuum energy $\sum_k \omega_k = \arg\det U$ vanishes. The pairing and $\det U = 1$ hold for **every** $g$: they are identities of the algebra, not properties of a chosen coupling.

**Theorem 4 (the matter/antimatter spectral mirror).** *Because every $T_{ab}$ is even, $[U,w] = 0$ to $10^{-15}$, so $U$ preserves the $16$ and $\overline{16}$ eigenspaces, and*

$$\omega_{\overline{16}} = -\omega_{16},\qquad \mathrm{spec}\big(U\big|_{\overline{16}}\big) = \mathrm{spec}\big(U\big|_{16}^\dagger\big),$$

*verified to $2\times10^{-15}$ over the grid $g \in \{0.05,\dots,0.5\}$.*

This is the honest content of "CPT" in this theory: a **spectral** statement — the antiparticle levels are the negatives of the particle levels, so the antimatter evolution has the same spectrum as the time-reversed matter evolution. It is not the full CPT theorem: no antiunitary time-reversal operator is constructed on the many-cell Hilbert space and no spin-statistics connection is derived. A unitary involution $C = h_1$ maps the $\mathbf{16}$ onto the $\overline{\mathbf{16}}$ (it anticommutes with $w$), but calling $C\cdot T$ "CPT" overstates what has been built.

The mechanism of the pairing is understood, and its scope is limited. The intertwiner between the $32$-dimensional module and its conjugate is unique (nullity $1$, rank $32$); it satisfies $Q h_a Q^\dagger = h_a^*$ to $2\times10^{-15}$ for the ten generators and $Q T_{ab} Q^\dagger = -T_{ab}^*$ to the same bound for the $45$, and the associated antiunitary $\mathcal J$ has $\mathcal J^2 = +1$. That is the content of $\mathrm{Cl}(10,0)\simeq M_{32}(\mathbb{R})$: $\mathrm{Spin}(10)$ lands in $\mathrm{SO}(32)$, and a real form of $\mathrm{U}(32)$ carries no CP-odd invariant. Over all eleven signatures $p+q = 10$ the Frobenius–Schur indicator is $+1$ for $p-q \equiv 0,2 \bmod 8$ and $-1$ for $p-q \equiv 4,6$ (validated on $\mathrm{Cl}(2,0) \simeq M_2(\mathbb{R})$ and $\mathrm{Cl}(0,2) \simeq \mathbb{H}$), so $\mathrm{Cl}(8,2)$, $\mathrm{Cl}(7,3)$, $\mathrm{Cl}(4,6)$, $\mathrm{Cl}(3,7)$, $\mathrm{Cl}(0,10)$ are quaternionic — and on the quaternionic root the $21$ mixed even products $h'_a h'_b$ are Hermitian traceless involutions generating an exactly unitary step ($6.7\times10^{-16}$, $\det U = 1$) whose spectrum is still exactly conjugation-closed ($3\times10^{-15}$): the mirror is not a privilege of reality. Two scope limits come with it. The antiunitary inverts every single factor ($\mathcal J F \mathcal J^{-1} = F^{-1}$) but reverses the order of the ordered product, so $\mathcal J U \mathcal J^{-1} \ne U^{-1}$ (deviation $0.58$ for $\mathrm{Cl}(7,3)$, $0.81$ for $\mathrm{Cl}(10,0)$): the pairing is a property of the whole ordered step. ### 7.3 Locality and the causal radius

**Theorem 5 (the causal radius is implemented).** *On an $(L_b,L_b,L_b)$ torus of cells the step is a product of two-cell operators: every bond stage couples sub-sites at Planck-cell distance $\le 1$ — an intra-block pair joins neighbouring sub-sites of one cell, an inter-block pair joins neighbouring cells. Hence after $n$ stages the support of a sub-site-localised excitation lies inside the $n$-cell light cone: at most one Planck cell per stage, and since three of the six stages are inter-block, at most three blocks — six Planck cells — per step. Numerically: the norm of a random cell state is preserved to $10^{-15}$; on an $8^3$-cell torus the support radius after $n$ stages is exactly $n$ Planck cells for every $n \le 12$ — the front *saturates* the cone, front speed $1.0$; on a $32^3$-cell torus run for $30$ stages the front reaches $21$ cells, and no site of the support and no amplitude ever appears outside the $n$-cell cone ($0$, to machine precision); the same cone is realised at $g = 0.3$ as at $1/5$; the internal layer alone, with the bond stages removed, keeps the excitation on one sub-site forever.*

The bond layer is therefore what turns the light cone of Section 3 into a propagation bound. This is the cleanest dynamical result of the theory.

The $n$-particle $S$-matrix of the automaton is $S^{(n)} = U^{\otimes n}$ on the antisymmetrised $n$-particle space: elastic, bounded, with cross-sections bounded by $4\pi/M_P^2$. ## 8. Lattice chirality and the doubling problem

The Nielsen–Ninomiya theorem forbids a single chiral fermion in a theory with a *local, translation-invariant, Hermitian* lattice Dirac operator and a bounded spectrum [19]. CL10 uses no lattice Dirac operator: chirality is a property of the on-site module (the pseudoscalar eigenspaces) and the update is a finite-dimensional unitary. The theorem's hypotheses are therefore not all present, and the theorem does not by itself forbid the construction.

That is *not* the same as having evaded it, and the question is decidable by computation. The step is one time-independent operator on the cell lattice, so the good quantum number is the **block crystal momentum** $q$ — the phase under a translation by one automaton cell, $2\pi$-periodic, zone $q \in [-\pi,\pi]^3$ — and the eight sub-sites are the internal structure of the Bloch matrix. The $16$-sector one-step matrix $M(q)$ is computed exactly at $128 \times 128$ and cross-checked against the real-space step on a $2^3$-block torus (agreement $10^{-14}$). The cubic high-symmetry momenta carry the whole spectrum: $\Gamma = (0,0,0)$; $\mathrm{X} = (\pm\tfrac\pi2,0,0)$, star $6$; $\mathrm{M} = (\pm\tfrac\pi2,\pm\tfrac\pi2,0)$, star $12$, little group $C_{2v}$; $\mathrm{R} = (\pm\tfrac\pi2,\pm\tfrac\pi2,\pm\tfrac\pi2)$, star $8$, little group $C_{3v}$.

**Proposition 2 (lattice chirality).** *(i) The $16$ sector is closed by the full step, not only by the internal layer:* the bond layer acts on the sub-site index and commutes with $w$, so a state initialised in the $\mathbf{16}$ leaves it by less than $10^{-15}$. Chirality is a dynamical symmetry of the automaton. *(ii) The zone centre is gapped:* $\min_a|\omega_a(q=0)| = 0.0365$ per step, so the would-be massless matter is **not** at $q = 0$. *(iii) The gapless points are the doublers:* the $16$-sector one-step spectrum is gapless at the $12$ momenta $(\pm\tfrac\pi2,\pm\tfrac\pi2,0)$ and their permutations — the $\mathrm{M}$ points of the block zone, at half-zone momentum, whose Planck-cell momenta are $p = q/2$ — with node chiralities summing to $0$, as Nielsen–Ninomiya requires; at $g = 1/5$ the nodes still carry a small gap, $2.0\times10^{-4}$. The gap at $q = 0$ is not protected: it moves from $0.036$ at $g = 0.2$ to $0.072$ at $g = 0.15$ and to $2.35$ at $g = 0.1$.*

The doubling therefore **survives** the automaton: "the $\mathbf{16}$ is a single chiral generation" is a property of the *internal module*, while the *lattice spectrum*, which is what couples to low-energy physics, contains twelve gapless nodes.

The remedy is part of the construction. A Wilson-type term built from the root's own algebra — an internal factor

with $X, Y$ the unification Cartan directions identified in Section 13.1 and $q$ the block momentum, applied once per step — multiplies the one-step matrix without touching its unitarity ($W(0) = I$, $\det = 1$; deformed step unitary to $3.3\times10^{-15}$) and leaves the bond stages strictly causal. The factor itself is not local: as a function of the block momentum it is a multiplication, so its real-space kernel is a Bessel tail in **block** displacement — $6.7\times10^{-2}$ at block radius $\ge 2$ and $2.2\times10^{-3}$ at $\ge 4$ for $w = 0.1$, and $1.8\times10^{-17}$ at $\ge 4$ at the physical strength. ### 8.3 The massless-mode census

The implemented step has **no exactly gapless mode anywhere**. The step factorises exactly as $M(q) = P(q) \otimes U_{16}^{6}$ ($5.4\times10^{-15}$), so any cubic symmetry acts on the $8$-dimensional bond factor alone and the internal $\mathbf{16}$ is inert. ## 9. The quantized clock: the root closes its own parameter

The parameter audit of Section 16 classes the rotation angle as the one free continuous parameter of the dynamics and classes the rule $g = 1/n_{\rm qubits}$ as a convention the data reject. There is a sharper condition, and the algebra itself enforces it. A cellular-automaton ontology demands that the fundamental update be *algebraic*, not analytic: the step must be an element of the root — a member of the Clifford group generated by the $h_a$, which for the plane-rotation product means $e^{ig}$ a root of unity. Call this the **closure axiom**: no continuous phase may enter the dynamics.

**Theorem 6 (the two closed steps).** *Over every rational angle $g = p\pi/q$ ($q \le 16$) and every candidate of the implemented rule ($1/5$, $\pi/5$, $\pi/10$, $\pi/8$, $3\pi/8$, $\pi/6$, $\pi/3$), the internal step $U(g)$ is exactly periodic at precisely two angles: $g = \pi/2$, of order $4$, and $g = \pi/4$, of order $8$. At the implemented $g = 1/5$ the internal clock is irrational: the smallest level is $0.011466$ rad per step, $2\pi/\omega_{\min} = 548.0$, and no order $\le 64$ exists (deviation $0.45$).*

*Proof of the $\pi/2$ case, and a stronger statement.* Each plane rotation at the half angle is $-iT_{ab}$, and $T_{ab} = i h_a h_b$, so $U(\pi/2) = (-i)^{45} i^{45} \prod_{a<b} h_a h_b = \prod_{a<b} h_a h_b$. The product of all $45$ pairs in ascending order reduces — numerically to $6.1\times10^{-17}$ in the $1024$-element algebra basis, with every other basis coefficient below $10^{-8}$ — to the single monomial $h_0 h_1 \cdots h_9$, the pseudoscalar $w$; since $w^2 = -I$, $U(\pi/2)^4 = I$. The identity is *ordering-independent*: the monomial content of any ordering of the $45$ factors is $\pm w$, because each $h_k$ appears in exactly nine pairs, and five shuffled orderings reproduce the order-$4$ to $9.8\times10^{-16}$. The step at the half angle *is* the root's chirality operator. $\blacksquare$

**The quantized step ($g = \pi/4$).** $U(\pi/4)^4 = -I$ and $U(\pi/4)^8 = I$ exactly ($5.0\times10^{-15}$), and the $32$ internal levels are exactly the algebraic set

$$\omega \in \{\pm\tfrac{\pi}{4}\ (10),\ \pm\tfrac{3\pi}{4}\ (6)\},\qquad \max_k\big|\omega_k - k\pi/4\big| < 3\times10^{-15},$$

$\pm$-paired, zero point below $2\times10^{-15}$, $\det U = 1$: the internal spectrum becomes pure algebra, with no continuous number anywhere. The invariants that hold for every $g$ (the $16/\overline{16}$ split, the mirror, $\det U = 1$) are untouched. The order-$8$ requires the canonical Appendix-A ordering of the $45$ factors — five shuffled orderings break it (deviation $\sim 1$) — so the ordering, already an input of the step, carries the quantization.

**The full step closes too.** At $g = \pi/4$ the exact one-step Bloch matrix of the $16$ sector at the zone centre satisfies $M(0)^2 = I$ below $1\times10^{-14}$ (and on the full $256$-dimensional module below $1\times10^{-14}$; $16$-sector closure leak below $5\times10^{-16}$): the fundamental dynamics is a finite-state machine, 't Hooft's determinism exact at the fundamental update. The doubler defect of Section 8.3 is repaired, but the repaired census is not a set of points. At $g = \pi/4$ the exactly gapless set of the block zone is $\Gamma$ together with the **six face-diagonal lines** $\{(\pm t, \pm t, 0)\}$ and its axis permutations: every point of every line is gapless below $7\times10^{-16}$ over its whole length, the twelve M points are only the high-symmetry points that happen to sit on them (their gaps $0$–$2.2\times10^{-16}$), X and R stay gapped at $0.5236$, the body diagonal is gapped away from $\Gamma$ ($0.022$ at $t = 0.3$), and none of the gapless points of a $15^3$ grid lies off the lines — where the implemented angle finds no gapless point at all. The step still factorises as $M(q) = P(q) \otimes U_{16}^{6}$ there ($4.0\times10^{-15}$). The touching is **quadratic**: through an M point the gap stays zero along the line, grows linearly in one transverse direction (slope $0.577$) and quadratically in the other, and the three axis exponents at every one of the twelve nodes are a permutation of $(1,1,2)$. The chirality bookkeeping of Proposition 2 then fails as stated: it needs isolated zeros, and at $12/12$ M nodes the gap along the line through the node stays below $10^{-9}$ at steps $10^{-3}, 10^{-2}, 10^{-1}$ — the charges whose sum is exactly $0$ at $g = 1/5$ become sign noise, and their naive sum is no longer quantized ($-2$ or $-6$ depending on the arithmetic route), so the Nielsen–Ninomiya count must be redone per line, not per node. The helicity question re-asked at the new gapless point answers as it did at an M node: the step's point group at $\pi/4$ is the same $16$-element index-$3$ subgroup of the $48$ signed permutations and $\Gamma$ is fixed by all of it; the exactly gapless subspace of $M(0)$ has dimension $64$ of $128$ (intertwiner leak below $10^{-13}$, $\langle\chi,\chi\rangle = 768$), and since the internal $\mathbf{16}$ is inert the content is that of the $4$-dimensional bond factor, whose isotypic constituents are four $1$-dimensional irreps — no $2$-dimensional irrep, hence no helicity $\pm1$ or $\pm2$ candidate at $\Gamma$ either.

### 9.1 The half-angle branch

At $g = \pi/2$ the six-stage bond matrix is diagonal to $1.2\times10^{-16}$ at every sampled momentum, its permutation part is the identity, and the eight sub-site phases are linear in $q$ with slopes the eight corner vectors $\{\pm1\}^3$: the bond layer is a pure lattice translation, no mixing, no interference. The half-angle branch has no mixing and therefore no mass generation: it is degenerate, and the finest closed clock $\pi/4$ is the physical branch.

### 9.2 What the closure costs

The loop machinery quantizes, and pays. The traced-link hop phases of Section 12.1 — built from the joint Casimir eigenvalues $-32,-12,+4$ — become exactly $\{1, i, i\}$ at $g = \pi/4$ (below $3\times10^{-15}$), and a new exact selection rule appears: $F_n = 0$ below $7\times10^{-15}$ for **every odd $n$** (the odd closed loops cancel identically; at $g = 1/5$ no such selection rule exists). The coupling convention pays too. At the closed angle the rotation-squared coupling is $\pi^2/16$, i.e. $\alpha = \pi/64 = 0.0491$ in the Standard Model normalisation, against the $1/25 = 0.04$ at which the desert solution meets (Section 13.1). Re-running the desert inverse problem at the quantized value with the same content still has a unique one-loop solution — $M_X = 1.235\times10^{17}$ GeV, $M_{10} = 7.1\times10^{4}$ GeV, $M_{(1,2)} = 4.9\times10^{17}$ GeV — but it is no longer a *desert* solution: the $(1,2)$ threshold sits **above** the unification scale, the threshold ordering the solution assumed is destroyed, and the proton lifetime jumps to $\tau_p = 2.8\times10^{39}$ yr. Conversely, holding $M_X$ at the data-preferred $1.8\times10^{16}$ GeV requires $\alpha_X = 0.040050$: the meeting coupling is rigidly tied to $M_X$ by that content, and the closed value is a factor $1.226$ off it. Against the one-loop target — the three couplings run to $M_P$, mean $0.023068$ — the closed value has relative gap $1.128$ where $1/25$ had $0.734$: the closure worsens the tension by a factor $1.54$ and sits $2.41\times$ above the data-optimal $1/7^2 = 0.0204$. The closed value is not of the $1/n^2$ form at all ($\pi/64 = 1/n^2$ needs $n = 4.51$; the nearest integers miss by $18.5\%$ and $27.3\%$).

**The verdict on the parameter criterion.** The closure axiom replaces the one free continuous parameter by a discrete index: the root forces $g \in \{\pi/2, \pi/4\}$, the half-angle branch degenerates, and the finest closed clock is the physical branch. ### 12.1 Where a loop factor can come from

The observed electroweak/Planck ratio is

### 13.3 Baryon asymmetry

The automaton alone cannot produce it. Every CP-odd (T-odd) invariant of the unitary step vanishes: $\mathrm{Im}\,\mathrm{Tr}$ of all two- and three-fold products of the $\mathbf{45}$, of a $2\times10^5$ sample of four-fold products, of all $10\times10\times45\times45$ baryon-violating sandwiches, and of the rank-1 real democratic Yukawa — all zero to $10^{-15}$. The step is unitary (in equilibrium), baryon-conserving (the $\mathbf{45}$ commute with $w$) and CP-conserving. It satisfies **none** of the Sakharov conditions [24]. This is a computed theorem, and it is the correct starting point. The theorem is stable under the Wilson extension: the extended step keeps the exact matter/antimatter spectral mirror (deviation $10^{-15}$), so the Wilson mechanism — powerful for unification and for the doublers — adds no CP phase and no Sakharov condition.

A GUT-leptogenesis estimate [39] is then built by hand:

$$\Delta B = \frac{28}{79}\,\varepsilon_{CP}\,D,\qquad \varepsilon_{CP} = \frac{g_0^4}{16\pi} = 4.9\times10^{-6},\qquad D = \Big(\frac{1}{16\pi}\Big)^2 = 3.96\times10^{-4},$$

with $g_0 = 1/2^3 = 1/8$ and the Standard Model sphaleron conversion $B = \tfrac{28}{79}(B-L)$ [25,26], giving $\Delta B = 6.8\times10^{-10}$ against the measured $(6.12\pm0.04)\times10^{-10}$ [14] — a ratio $1.11$.

Three problems. (i) $\varepsilon_{CP}$ is not a computed vertex: the physical CP asymmetry of $N\to \ell H$ is a one-loop self-energy/vertex function of the heavy-neutrino masses, and $g_0^4/16\pi$ is a dimensional guess. (ii) The out-of-equilibrium condition **fails** at the model's own couplings: at $M_N = 9.6\times10^{13}$ GeV, $H = 1.66\sqrt{g_*}M_N^2/M_P = 1.3\times10^{10}$ GeV while $\Gamma_N = y^2 M_N/8\pi = 6.0\times10^{10}$ GeV for $y = 1/8$ ($1.9\times10^{12}$ GeV for $y = 0.71$), so $\Gamma_N/H \simeq 4.6$ ($\simeq 148$). Out-of-equilibrium decay requires $y \lesssim 0.06$, which the theory does not select. (iii) $28/79$ is an input of the Standard Model sector, not a number of the root. The $11\%$ agreement is therefore a coincidence of chosen factors (O5). The failure is quantified through the washout of the same decay: at the self-consistent atmospheric Yukawa ($y = 0.40$, the one that gives $m_\nu = 0.0501$ eV) $\Gamma_N/H = 47$ — in equilibrium, Sakharov's third condition fails — and the physical strong-washout efficiency $\kappa_f = 5.4\times10^{-3}$ [39] sits $14\times$ away from the guessed dilution $(1/16\pi)^2$: the agreement is an accident of two wrong factors, not a near-miss.

A sharper statement is available. At the model's own seesaw scale, the out-of-equilibrium condition $\Gamma_N/H < 1$ gives $y < 0.058$. The same Yukawa fixes the light neutrino mass through the same seesaw,

$$m_\nu = \frac{y^2 v^2}{M_N} \le 2.1\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{eV}\qquad (y \le 0.058),$$

a bound of the same kind and size as the standard thermal-leptogenesis bound [32]. The atmospheric splitting requires $m_\nu \simeq 0.050$ eV, i.e. $y \simeq 0.28$ with $m_D = yv$ and $v = 246$ GeV ($y \simeq 0.40$ in the $v_u = 174$ GeV convention), for which $\Gamma_N/H \simeq 23$. **At $M_N = 9.6\times10^{13}$ GeV the neutrino mass and the out-of-equilibrium condition are mutually incompatible**: the framework cannot have both the seesaw scale it needs for the ladder and the leptogenesis it needs for the asymmetry. (The model's own ladder sits at $m_D = 118$ GeV, i.e. $y = 0.48$, where $\Gamma_N/H \simeq 68$.)

The one route the framework supplies is quasi-degenerate (resonant) leptogenesis [31,40]. The democratic structure places the three heavy singlets on the *same* ladder rung, hence degenerate to the accuracy of whatever breaks the family symmetry — precisely the resonant setup, where the CP asymmetry is enhanced by near-degeneracy instead of by a large Yukawa. With $y = 1/16\pi = 0.0199$ the decay is out of equilibrium ($\Gamma_N/H = 0.12$), and the asymmetry that the model's own dilution factor and sphaleron conversion require, $\varepsilon_{CP} = \Delta B/[(28/79)D] = 4.4\times10^{-6}$, is *smaller* than the natural one-loop size $y^2/16\pi = 7.9\times10^{-6}$ at that Yukawa: the bookkeeping closes at the scale of the numbers, and the degeneracy the resonant regime needs, $\Delta M/M \lesssim \Gamma_N/M_N = y^2/8\pi = 1.6\times10^{-5}$, is what the parameter-free democratic base gives exactly and what the family-breaking perturbation must not overshoot. The obstacle is the phase, not the size: the model's Yukawa is real, every CP-odd invariant of the step vanishes, and a strictly degenerate spectrum has no CP-violating phase left to enhance. This is a *route*, not a result.

The phase, however, is no longer the missing ingredient. The mirror is exactly the structure that forbids CP: every mirror-preserving extension (the Wilson factor: pairing deviation $2.1\times10^{-15}$) leaves the CP phase at zero, while the most general per-class $\kappa_s$ layer and the most general real-symmetric bond generator destroy the $\pm$ pairing (deviation $\sim 1$, $60$ draws, both mirror sign conventions) — a CP phase costs Theorem 4. But the prohibition is a statement about the *on-site* module (Section 7.2), and the bond layer is not covered by it: a Peierls flux $A_1 = \Phi x_0$ on the inter-block bonds is exactly unitary ($4.8\times10^{-15}$), keeps $\det U = 1$ at every flux ($5.3\times10^{-15}$), commutes with the pseudoscalar (mirror $\mathbb{Z}_2$ leak $3.3\times10^{-16}$) and keeps the hop at radius $1$ ($2.6\times10^{-16}$), while moving the band spectrum by up to $0.70$; its gauge-invariant strength, the plaquette holonomy, has $\operatorname{Im} = 0$ at $\Phi = 0$ and $1.6\times10^{-3}$ at $\Phi = \pi/2$. ## 14. Cosmology

### 14.1 Dark energy

The vacuum energy of the automaton vanishes at the level of the internal (one-sub-site) spectrum: the $32$ levels are $\pm$-paired for every coupling, so $\sum_k\omega_k = \arg\det U = 0$. This is the correct, non-vacuous form of the cancellation. (Counting the $512$ even and $512$ odd **elements of the algebra** as bosonic and fermionic zero-point modes is not a physical mode counting — the physical single-sub-site Hilbert space has $32$ states, not $1024$ — and is not used.)

A vanishing tree-level vacuum energy is technically natural: no relevant operator is generated if the spectral pairing is an exact symmetry. The pairing survives the Wilson extension (the extended spectrum stays $\pm$-paired to $10^{-15}$ and the internal zero point stays $0$, because the Wilson direction is a traceless Cartan combination), so the new ingredient neither breaks the zero nor moves it.

The zero is **protected, not accidental**. Deform the step by $50$ random traceless Hermitian generators, $U \to e^{i\varepsilon A}U$, at strengths up to $\varepsilon = 1$: the quasi-energy sum stays pinned to zero, $|S| < 4\times10^{-14}$, because $\det U = 1$ makes $\sum_k\omega_k = \arg\det U$ a topological quantity, and no branch crossing occurs below $\varepsilon = \pi$. The only internal knob that moves the sum is the $\mathrm{U}(1)$ trace phase.

What the theory does **not** do is produce the observed value. The observed $\Lambda \sim 10^{-122}M_P^4$ lies $122$ orders of magnitude *below* the classical bound $M_P^4$ and *above* the theory's spectral zero, and no mechanism breaking the pairing by that amount is known. Independently, the classical self-gravitating de Sitter vacuum of Section 11 has $G_{\mu\nu} = -3H^2 g_{\mu\nu}$, i.e. a vacuum energy bounded by $M_P^4$ on the lattice. These are three different statements — spectral zero, classical bound, observed value — and they do not "reconcile": the gap between the first and the third is O1, the largest unexplained number in the theory [37]. The equation of state of a cosmological-constant vacuum is $w = -1$ by definition, not by derivation.

The problem is now a single named number: the observed $\Lambda$ is **equivalent to** a trace-phase breaking of size

$$\varepsilon\operatorname{Tr}A \sim 16\pi^2\times10^{-122} = 1.6\times10^{-120}.$$

O1 is no longer "why is the vacuum energy small" but "what breaks the trace $\mathrm{U}(1)$ by $10^{-120}$".

### 14.2 Dark matter

The candidate supplied by the classical sector is the kink of Section 11.4. It is static, gauge-neutral and finite-energy **per unit area**, but it is a domain wall, not a localised particle; a network of Planck-scale walls would dominate the energy density and is cosmologically excluded. The quantum-sector candidates have been scanned (Section 12.2): the exact mirror $\mathbb{Z}_2$ of the step is the only exact-stability structure in the implemented spectrum; no Wilson-split node can host a neutral mirror at the ladder mass without also delivering a stable coloured or charged partner; and the mirror sector's own lightest states sit at $1.3\times10^{8}$ GeV (coloured, excluded) and $2\times10^{10}$ GeV — not at the ladder's $118$ GeV. The theory supplies no dark-matter *particle* within the implemented spectrum; the minimal named extension is the mirror-odd ladder scalar of Section 12.2. The mass prediction stands, the carrier is an open problem (O7).

**The relic abundance is computed, and it is not the observed one.** Solving the freeze-out integral

$$\Omega h^2 = \frac{1.07\times10^{9}\ \mathrm{GeV}\ x_f}{\sqrt{g_*}\,M_P\langle\sigma v\rangle},\qquad \langle\sigma v\rangle = a + \frac{6b}{x_f},$$

reproduces the standard weakly-interacting-massive-particle curve: $m = 100$ GeV with $\sigma v = 3\times10^{-26}$ cm$^3$/s gives $\Omega h^2 = 0.080$ [41]. At the ladder mass $m = 118.5$ GeV the measured $\Omega h^2 = 0.120$ requires $\sigma v = 1.98\times10^{-26}$ cm$^3$/s, i.e. an effective coupling $\alpha_{\rm eff} = 0.0086$ in $\sigma v = \alpha^2/(\pi m^2)$ — while the root supplies only $g^2/4\pi = 0.0032$ (giving $\Omega h^2 = 0.798$) or the loop factor $1/16\pi = 0.0199$ ($\Omega h^2 = 0.0246$). The automaton's own light spectrum enters at the same order: its twelve near-gapless nodes are nodal **planes** (Section 8.2) with mean speed $0.201$, so the final-state density is enhanced by

$$D = \sum \frac{1}{\lvert v\rvert^3} = 1485$$

over the isotropic estimate — a correction the $(100/m)^3$ scaling of the standard curve cannot see — and with it the two supplied couplings give $\Omega h^2 = 2.3\times10^{-5}$ and $7.6\times10^{-4}$. The named portals of Section 12.2 reproduce $0.120$ only because their coupling was inverted from the fiducial $3\times10^{-26}$: computed forward they give $0.081$ and $0.156$, and $0.000$ once the nodal-plane density is included. **The relic abundance is a computed number now, and it is not $0.120$ for any coupling the root supplies.** Non-thermal production (freeze-in) is not considered here [42].

### 14.3 Antimatter

## 19. Discussion

**What is genuinely new.** Not the $\mathbf{16}$ of $\mathrm{Spin}(10)$, not the $5/3$, not anomaly cancellation: those are fifty-year-old results of the grand-unification literature [6–9,12]. The gauge sector gets the normalisation right and, under desert running, the values wrong; the repair restores unification at $\alpha_X = 1/25$ exactly and its *pattern* is a consequence of the automaton (the split doublers of the Wilson mechanism, the breaking chain, the $n_{\text{nodes}} \ge 3$ bound), but the mechanism's own root meets at a scale excluded by proton decay, no redistribution of node strengths can move it, and the surviving solution keeps two fitted thresholds and abandons $M_{\rm GUT} = M_P$. The supplier question is closed on three fronts — the gauged layer's composite matter is excluded, the ansatz families leave residuals $5.7$–$6.1$, and the equal-shift identity is a theorem over complete multiplets — while the constructive junction route fabricates the right fragment pattern and misses the meeting at a floor that the general junction does not improve. The flavour-dependent bond layer removes the obstruction but is an extension with six named inputs. The flavour sector derives one angle out of six and none of the masses. The dark-matter carrier cannot exist inside the implemented spectrum, and the built minimal fix is a named tension between exact mirror stability and the observed abundance. The loop factor is reproduced to $3.6\%$ by closed traced loops at exactly the ladder length, but the single-particle renormalisation group provably composes angles without dividing them, the gauged layer's level-1 division falls short by a factor $26$ and does not compound, and the counted loops miss the neutrino-ladder exponent by a factor $0.066$.

## Appendix A. The explicit 32-dimensional representation

$$g_{2k} = Y^{\otimes k}\otimes X\otimes I^{\otimes(4-k)},\qquad g_{2k+1} = Y^{\otimes k}\otimes Z\otimes I^{\otimes(4-k)}.$$

On the same qubit slot $X$ and $Z$ anticommute; across slots, the $Y^{\otimes k}$ prefix makes a generator at slot $k$ anticommute with any generator at slot $j<k$.
